Instant Expert

A five-minute brief so you can talk to a agribusiness & food prospect like someone who understands their world — not a caller reading a script.

The labour market they live in

Regional WA businesses face the hardest labour market of all — small local populations, seasonal demand and strong competition for both field and office staff. Attracting an experienced administrator to a regional town is difficult and expensive, so owners and coordinators end up doing the office work themselves on top of operations.

Why the admin load hurts

Food and agribusiness run on documentation: export and logistics paperwork, supplier and customer follow-up, inventory and order administration, bookkeeping, seasonal workforce admin and marketing. During harvest, season or export windows this all peaks at once, and lean teams simply can't keep up — orders slip, documents delay shipments and cash flow suffers.

The pressures shaping their year

Work is intensely seasonal, margins are exposed to commodity prices and freight costs, and export compliance is exacting. Peaks demand surge capacity that's uneconomic to hire locally year-round, while off-peak periods can't justify permanent office headcount — a structural mismatch offshore support fits well.

Where offshore support fits

Growers, production, quality and operational decisions stay with the local team. A dedicated offshore administrator handles the recurring office work — export docs, logistics admin, supplier/customer follow-up, inventory, bookkeeping support and marketing coordination — giving surge capacity through peaks without a permanent local overhead.

Talk like an insider

Know the language: export documentation, phytosanitary/health certificates, incoterms, bills of lading, packing lists, cold chain, wholesale and distribution, cellar door, seasonal labour. Know the tools: Xero/MYOB, CargoWise-adjacent, inventory and winery systems. Speak to surge capacity and follow-up discipline.

Qualification questions

  • Can I ask, does your local team spend much time on logistics or supplier follow-up?
  • Are export documents, customer updates or inventory administration handled manually?

  • Would a dedicated support person be useful if they were trained in your systems and processes?

Objection handling — agribusiness & food

How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting for the Commercial Manager, not winning the point on the call.
ObjectionResponse angle
“Our work is hands-on / in the field.”Field, production and quality stay with your team. We support the office trail — export docs, logistics, follow-up, inventory. Which backs up most in season?
“We're regional and small.”That's exactly the fit — offshore support gives you administrative capacity you can't easily hire regionally, without a permanent local overhead.
“Export paperwork is exacting.”Which is why a trained, consistent resource who learns your documents helps. You keep ownership of compliance; the processing gets reliable.
“We tried outsourcing.”Usually scoping and supervision. Our managed model trains and supervises the role and covers leave — not a gig marketplace.
“It's very seasonal.”The strength of the model — surge capacity through harvest or export windows without hiring and shedding local staff.
“Send me info.”Happy to — is it export docs, logistics or bookkeeping that hurts most? I'll send the right example.
Know when to stop. A clear "no" frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and respect every opt-out immediately.
