1.1 · Purpose of this playbook

The shared picture — the purpose of the role, the systems that govern every decision, the conduct and confidentiality it demands, and the rhythms that keep it predictable.

This playbook exists to provide certainty. It removes ambiguity from the day, defines how work flows through the Executive Assistant role, and establishes what is owned, to what standard, and when to escalate.

Executive support fails when it relies on memory, goodwill or constant verbal instruction. This playbook replaces those habits with structure — a rhythm that is predictable, traceable and repeatable, so good outcomes don’t depend on anyone remembering the right thing at the right moment. It is written for one role, today filled by Jho, supporting both directors.

Who it applies to

The Executive Assistant role in all its work — normal weeks, peak demand and the directors’ travel, regardless of workload pressure or competing priorities.

When you may deviate

Only where a director instructs it, or where confidentiality, safety or a genuine emergency requires it. Every deviation is noted and reviewed — never made quietly.

What it is not

Not a list of everything that could ever be asked. It sets how the role works and the standard it holds — the specific tasks grow and shift as trust deepens.

The aimGive the directors back the one thing they can’t make more of — their attention — by handling everything else reliably, kindly, and without being asked twice.

1.4 · The five levels of trust

Delegation isn’t all-or-nothing — it grows as trust does. Every work category in part two climbs the same five levels. The role operates at Gatekeeper (Level 3) by default; individual categories may sit higher or lower by agreement with the directors.

1Reliable hands

Works from instructions on everyday, low-stakes tasks. Output stays visible; nothing needs the keys to anything sensitive.

2Standing access

Recurring access to calendar, inbox and systems, applying light judgement to keep the routine moving without being asked twice.

3GatekeeperCurrent default

Represents and filters on the director’s behalf. Decides what reaches them, speaks for the office in day-to-day matters, and protects their time and focus.

4Delegated authority

Makes decisions within agreed guardrails and handles sensitive, financial and confidential matters with discretion. The directors set the boundaries; the role owns what’s inside them.

5Full trust

Acts on the director’s behalf on confidential, strategic and fiduciary matters — a genuine extension of them, trusted to commit in their name.

From capacity to capabilityAt the first levels the role unlocks capacity — handing back hours lost to admin. As trust deepens it extends capability: work done in the directors’ name reaches further than they could alone. The value isn’t the tasks ticked off — it’s the leverage the role creates.

1.5 · Standards of conduct

The Executive Assistant represents the directors’ office in everything they do. The level of communication and professionalism must be exemplary at all times — people form an impression of Tony, Alexis and Brainbox through every exchange with this role.

Written & spoken English

Clear, correct and warm in every register — email, message, phone and in person. Grammar, spelling and tone checked before anything goes out. Australian English; no slang when representing the office.

Professionalism

Punctual, reliable and composed — especially under pressure. Commitments met or renegotiated early, never dropped silently. Discretion, courtesy and good judgement in every interaction.

Presentation & dress

Professional and well-presented at all times, appropriate to representing the directors — lifted further for client-facing days, events and meetings where the role is the face of the office.

Always Be Kind

Brainbox’s core value upheld in every exchange — with directors, colleagues, clients and suppliers alike. Firm when needed, always kind.

1.6 · Confidentiality & information security

This role is regularly in receipt of sensitive information. Protecting it is not a courtesy — it is the foundation of the trust the role rests on. Read this section as binding.

The absolute ruleAll personal data relating to the directors is considered highly sensitive and is not to be shared with any other party — including those who claim a “right to know”.

Gossip is prohibited

Speculating about, repeating or trading in information about the directors, staff or clients is strictly prohibited — in the office, online, or socially. What is learned through the role stays within the role.

Screen & casual observance

Position screens away from passers-by; lock the device when stepping away; use a privacy filter in shared spaces. Sensitive matters are not discussed where they can be overheard.

Document handling & storage

Sensitive documents are stored only in approved, access-controlled locations — never on personal devices. Printing avoided; paper filed securely or shredded. Clean desk, always.

Access points & tools

Strong, unique passwords and MFA; credentials never shared. No confidential or personal director data is entered into AI tools or any unapproved third-party service.

Serious misconductUnauthorised disclosure will be treated as Serious Misconduct. If anyone — staff, family, supplier, caller or colleague — presses for the directors’ personal information, the answer is a polite decline and an escalation to the director. If ever unsure whether something may be shared, say nothing and ask the relevant director first.

1.7 · Operating philosophy & escalation

A few principles sit underneath every decision. They explain why the role is shaped the way it is — and how a request should move from the moment it arrives.

Protect their attention

The directors’ focus is the scarcest resource. Default to handling, not forwarding. Only what genuinely needs them reaches them.

Log once, track to close

Requests are captured once in Asana, tracked until closed, and reviewed for patterns. Nothing lives only in memory or an inbox.

Anticipate, don’t react

Look ahead and remove problems before they land. The best work is the friction the directors never feel in the first place.

How a request moves

Step 1 · Intake
Routed to Jho

Requests funnel to one point of contact — nothing handled off the books.

Step 2 · Log
Logged in Asana

A clear, categorised task with context — verbal becomes written.

Step 3 · Decide
Handle, refer or escalate

Judged against the agreed level and guardrails.

Step 4 · Act
Actioned or raised

Resolved within authority, or put to the right director with options.

Step 5 · Close
Closed & confirmed

Closed only on confirmation; patterns reviewed so it doesn’t recur.

Handle · refer · escalateHandle within the agreed level, then note by exception. Refer what’s better owned elsewhere, tracked to close. Escalate what needs a director’s call — early, with context and options, not just a question.

1.8 · Daily, weekly & monthly rhythm

The role runs to a known shape: clear risk early, lock the day once, protect focus, and close cleanly — with the week and month layered on top.

The dayWhat runs
Start of dayScan both directors’ inboxes and calendars; flag anything urgent. Prepare a short morning brief for each.
MorningClear the inbox to plan; action and log requests in Asana; confirm the day’s meetings, prep and travel. Protect focus blocks.
Through the dayFilter what reaches the directors; run meeting prep and follow-ups; handle correspondence in their voice; keep tasks moving to close.
End of dayTidy the queue, roll open items forward visibly, and prepare tomorrow’s look-ahead so the day closes without loose ends.

The week

Mon plan the week; confirm meetings, travel and priorities. Tue–Thu execute — meetings, follow-ups, projects, research, correspondence. Fri close out, reconcile, and prepare next week’s look-ahead for each director.

The month

Reconcile expenses in Dext and close the month’s spend; look ahead a month — travel, events, deadlines, key dates; refresh stakeholder, gifting and wellbeing touches due; review what recurred and refine systems.

1.10 · Non-negotiable rules & outcome

  1. All Director personal data is highly sensitive — never disclosed, even to those claiming a right to know. Unauthorised disclosure is Serious Misconduct.
  2. Communication, professionalism and presentation are exemplary at all times — Always Be Kind in every exchange.
  3. Asana is the only work register — if it isn’t logged, it isn’t actioned. Outlook is the only calendar authority.
  4. The role acts within its agreed trust level — never committing in a director’s name beyond it without approval.
  5. Spend follows the Procurement Policy and the directors’ limits; expenses are captured in Dext, never improvised.
  6. Where the two directors’ needs compete, the clash is surfaced — never resolved silently in favour of one.
Operating outcomeWhen it is followed, the role becomes predictable. Predictability builds trust, and trust is what lets the role grow — from reliable hands to a genuine extension of the directors. Structure is what lets us be kind under pressure, and calm systems make room for it.

4.2 · Asana conventions

Asana is the primary work platform of the role. All activities are recorded and tracked here — it is what makes the role visible, accountable and safe to hand more to over time.

Every task carries

Granular detail (anyone could pick it up) · a due date (realistic, kept current, never blank) · accurate status (reflects reality at any moment) · a next step (what happens next).

The rule

If it isn’t in Asana, it isn’t happening. A task gives every piece of work an owner, a history and a finish line — that’s what makes the role accountable rather than just busy.

Good practice

  • Log verbal requests as tasks before acting
  • One task per piece of work — no duplicates
  • Attach context, files and photos where useful
  • Update status & next step as things move
  • Close only on confirmation, not assumption

Avoid

  • Work held only in your head or an inbox
  • Tasks with no owner, date or detail
  • Stale statuses that no longer reflect reality
  • Duplicate or vague “catch-all” tasks
  • Confidential detail where it shouldn’t sit

4.3 · Escalation matrix

For every request, the question is the same: handle, refer, or escalate? This is the quick guide — when unsure, escalate.

Handle — within level

When: routine, within the agreed Gatekeeper level and any spend limits. Examples: scheduling, routine replies, bookings, minor purchases, briefs, standard supplier orders.

Refer — right owner

When: better owned by someone else — Finance, Facilities Manager, IT, an adviser. Examples: facilities work, payroll/finance queries, IT issues, legal questions.

Escalate — director’s call

When: beyond the agreed level, sensitive, above spend limits, or a clash between the two directors. Examples: disclosure requests, significant spend, commitments in a director’s name.

How to escalate wellRaise it early, to the right director, with context and options — not just a question. For anything touching confidentiality, the default is to say nothing and ask first.
