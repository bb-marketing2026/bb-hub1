The currency of the Philippines is the Philippine peso (PHP).
Some ATMs accept international credit and debit cards. Shops in towns and cities usually accept international
credit cards. If you’re travelling outside major cities, take sufficient funds in local currency with you. There may
not be ATMs or currency exchange facilities at your destination.
Cash in pounds sterling, euros or US dollars can be exchanged for Philippine pesos at the airport, in banks,
hotels and some shops.

Filipino food is a diverse and vibrant cuisine characterised by bold flavors and a fusion of influences. It
features a combination of indigenous, Malay, Spanish, Chinese, and American culinary traditions. Filipino
dishes often include rice as a staple, accompanied by a variety of meats, seafood, vegetables, and tropical
fruits. Filipino food celebrates the country’s rich cultural heritage and offers a delightful mix of sweet, sour, and
savory flavors.
Adobo
Philippine adobo is a traditional Filipino dish known for its tangy and
savory flavor. It is typically made by marinating meat, often chicken or
pork, in a mixture of vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and spices, and then
slow-cooking it until tender.
Sinigang
Sinigang is a traditional Filipino soup characterized by its sour and
savory flavor. It typically consists of meat (commonly pork, beef, or
shrimp) cooked in a tamarind-based broth, along with various
vegetables like tomatoes, onions, and leafy greens.

The Philippines offers stunning beach tourist destinations known for their pristine white sands, crystal-clear
waters, and vibrant marine life. From the famous Boracay Island with its lively atmosphere and water sports
activities to the secluded beauty of Palawan’s El Nido, visitors can indulge in sun-soaked relaxation, explore
breathtaking coral reefs, and enjoy breathtaking sunsets. Other notable beach destinations include Siargao,
renowned for its world-class surfing waves. The Philippines truly offers a tropical paradise for beach lovers.
Philippines Tourist Destinations
Kakanin
Kakanin is a traditional Filipino term used to refer to a variety of native
rice-based desserts. These delicacies are typically made from glutinous
or sticky rice, coconut milk, and sugar, and often feature vibrant colors
and unique textures. Kakanin is a popular part of Filipino cuisine and is
enjoyed as a sweet treat or a snack during special occasions, festivals,
and family gatherings.
Halo-halo
Halo-halo is a popular Filipino dessert that combines a colorful
assortment of ingredients such as sweetened fruits, beans, jellies, and
shaved ice, topped with evaporated milk and sometimes ice cream.

7	Brainbox Client Travel Guide
What to bring and prepare
Your passport must have at least six month’s remaining validity to be
admitted to the Philippines.
A visa is not required on short trips to the Philippines for either
business or tourism purposes.
You may only register with this link: https://etravel.gov.ph within 72
hours prior to your arrival or departure in the Philippines. Travelers
are enjoined to present their eTravel QR code to flight boarding.
Face masks are not mandatory, however it is adviseable to carry one
in case you may be near someone coughing or feel unwell yourself.
Cash machines dispensing local currency are available everywhere,
but please make sure you let your bank know that you will be
travelling.
Prepare your itinerary ahead of time. Please communicate your
plans to the Client Service Manager weeks prior to your visit –
especially when you plan to see local places over weekends or have
team meetings booked for you and your team members.
Philippines weather can be quite humid, so business clothing is
normally long pants or skirt and a polo shirt or blouse.
The Philippines is a conservative society and clothing after hours
should still be modest and not overly-revealing.
Each country has different restrictions on medication. If you have
prescribed medicines, please bring the prescriptions and make sure
they are permitted in the Philippines.
Most sockets can accept both Australian and American plugs, and
adapters are readily available in the street if required.
