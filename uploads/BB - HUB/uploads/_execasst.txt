1 . 1 · T H E O P E R A T I N G M A N U A L
Purpose of this playbook
This playbook exists to provide certainty. It removes ambiguity from the day, defines how work flows through
the Executive Assistant role, and establishes what is owned, to what standard, and when to escalate.
Executive support fails when it relies on memory, goodwill or constant verbal instruction. This playbook replaces those
habits with structure — a rhythm that is predictable, traceable and repeatable, so good outcomes don’t depend on anyone
remembering the right thing at the right moment. It is written for one role, today filled by Jho, supporting both directors.
W H O I T A P P L I E S T O
The Executive Assistant role in all its work — during normal weeks, peak demand and the directors’ travel, regardless of
workload pressure or competing priorities.
W H E N Y O U M A Y D E V I A T E
Only where a director instructs it, or where confidentiality, safety or a genuine emergency requires it. Every deviation is
noted and reviewed — never made quietly.
W H A T I T I S N O T
Not a list of everything that could ever be asked. It sets how the role works and the standard it holds — the specific tasks
grow and shift as trust deepens.
T H E A I M
Give the directors back the one thing they can’t make more of — their attention
— by handling everything else reliably, kindly, and without being asked twice.

1 . 4 · T H E O P E R A T I N G M A N U A L
The five levels of trust
Delegation isn’t all-or-nothing — it grows as trust does. Every work category in part two climbs the same five levels. The role
operates at Gatekeeper (Level 3) by default; individual categories may sit higher or lower by agreement with the directors.
1 Reliable hands
Works from instructions on everyday, low-stakes tasks. Output stays visible; nothing needs the keys to anything sensitive.
2 Standing access
Recurring access to calendar, inbox and systems, applying light judgement to keep the routine moving without being asked
twice.
4 Delegated authority
Makes decisions within agreed guardrails and handles sensitive, financial and confidential matters with discretion. The
directors set the boundaries; the role owns what’s inside them.
5 Full trust
Acts on the director’s behalf on confidential, strategic and fiduciary matters — a genuine extension of them, trusted to
commit in their name.
F R O M C A P A C I T Y T O C A P A B I L I T Y
At the first levels the role unlocks capacity — handing back hours lost to admin. As trust deepens it extends capability: work
done in the directors’ name reaches further than they could alone. The value isn’t the tasks ticked off — it’s the leverage the
role creates.

1 . 6 · T H E O P E R A T I N G M A N U A L
Confidentiality & information security
This role is regularly in receipt of sensitive information. Protecting it is not a courtesy — it is the foundation of the trust the
role rests on. Read this section as binding.
T H E A B S O L U T E R U L E
All personal data relating to the directors is considered highly sensitive and
is not to be shared with any other party — including those who claim a “right
to know”.
Gossip is prohibited
Speculating about, repeating or trading in information about
the directors, staff or clients is strictly prohibited — in the
office, online, or socially. What is learned through the role
stays within the role.
Screen & casual observance
Position screens away from passers-by; lock the device
when stepping away; use a privacy filter in shared or public
spaces. Sensitive matters are not discussed where they can
be overheard.
Document handling & storage
Sensitive documents are stored only in approved, access-
controlled locations — never on personal devices or
accounts. Printing is avoided; paper is filed securely or
shredded. Clean desk, always.
Access points & tools
Strong, unique passwords and MFA; credentials never
shared. No confidential or personal director data is entered
into AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT) or any unapproved third-
party service.
S E R I O U S M I S C O N D U C T
Unauthorised disclosure of confidential or personal director information will be treated as Serious Misconduct. If you
are ever unsure whether something may be shared, the answer is to say nothing and ask the relevant director first.
B R A I N B O X G R O U P · E X E C U T I V E A S S I S T A N T P L A Y B O O K 	1 . 6 · C O N F I D E N T I A L I T Y & I N F O R M A T I O N S E C U R I T Y

polite decline and an escalation to the director. The role never decides on its own that disclosure is acceptable.

Reporting lines & boundaries
Because the role straddles so many functions, its reporting line and boundaries need to be unusually clear — and gently
protected day to day.
R E P O R T S T O T H E D I R E C T O R S O N L Y
This role reports to the directors — Tony and Alexis — and not to local managers
or any other personnel.
Because the role works across so many functions, there is an ever-present risk that local reporting or accountability creeps
in. Any attempt to circumvent this line — or to introduce any form of local reporting or local accountability — is politely
referred to the directors. The role takes direction on tasks from many people; it answers to only two.
— Respect for leadership
Representing the directors’ interests does not make this a
manager-level role. Take care to engage in every manager-
level interaction respectfully and mindful of their
leadership positions in the company — influence comes
through service and courtesy, never status.
— Friendships & boundaries
The directors encourage workplace friendships — and
they’re welcome. This role must stay particularly vigilant
about actual and perceived boundaries and
confidentiality at all times: warmth with colleagues never
softens what is held in confidence.
