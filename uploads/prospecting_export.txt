Brainbox WA Offshoring Industry Prospecting Export
Prepared for Brainbox. Export begins with the query about Western Australian industries likely to be receptive to offshore personnel and includes subsequent prompts and responses. The prospect list is a first-pass public-source target list and should be verified in HubSpot before outreach.
Date: 2 July 2026
1. Industry selection query and response
User query
Identify the ten Western Australian industries most likely to be receptive to offshore personnel, and in a niche that is not already saturated or dominated by a single large player.
ChatGPT response
The best targets are not the obvious large employers. They are WA industries with many owner-managed or mid-market operators, high administrative load, tight local labour supply, and work that can be separated from the licensed, client-facing or site-based function.
The ten recommended industries are set out below. The ranking prioritises local labour pressure, fragmentation, buyer accessibility, clarity of offshore-suitable roles and the absence of a single dominant market player.
Rank
Industry niche
Best offshore roles
Saturation / dominance view
1
Residential builders, renovation firms and specialist trade contractors
Estimating support, take-offs, procurement admin, supplier follow-up, scheduling, maintenance coordination, CRM updates and invoice chasing.
Fragmented at the SME level. Avoid major builders and target 10 to 80 person builders, renovators and trade contractors.
2
Allied health, NDIS and private specialist clinics
Intake admin, appointment scheduling, NDIS plan admin, Medicare and private billing support, document formatting, referral follow-up and debtor support.
Not dominated at the small provider level. Target speech therapy, occupational therapy, psychology, physiotherapy, podiatry and multi-disciplinary clinics.
3
Mining services and METS SMEs
Procurement support, maintenance planning admin, HSEQ document control, mobilisation admin, timesheet processing, tender support and reporting.
Avoid mining majors. Target suppliers to mining because they have volume, urgency and fewer internal shared-service resources.
4
Engineering, surveying, planning and environmental consultancies
CAD and Revit drafting, GIS mapping support, report formatting, data clean-up, tender documentation, QA checklists, project admin and CRM support.
Fragmented below tier-one firms. Target 5 to 50 person consultancies with recurring project deadlines.
5
Transport, logistics and warehousing SMEs
Dispatch admin, proof-of-delivery processing, customer updates, compliance registers, fatigue documentation support, invoicing and fleet maintenance admin.
Fragmented outside major carriers. Target regional freight, specialised logistics, courier networks, cold chain, machinery transport and mining-supply logistics.
6
Insurance brokerages and financial services support firms
Renewal preparation, certificate processing, policy collation, claims admin, CRM hygiene, insurer follow-up, debtor support and onboarding packs.
Not dominated at SME brokerage level. Strong controls are needed around privacy, licensing boundaries and client communications.
7
RTOs, training providers and education services businesses
Enrolment admin, student support, LMS administration, document control, survey follow-up, compliance evidence collation and certificate preparation.
Private RTOs, niche training providers and workplace training businesses are better targets than universities or TAFEs.
8
Strata, property management and facilities services
Maintenance coordination, tenant and owner communications, arrears follow-up, lease admin, contractor compliance checks, inspection booking and invoice coding.
Fragmented at agency, strata and facilities-provider level. Focus on recurring operational administration.
9
Cybersecurity, MSPs and business IT providers
Level 1 helpdesk, ticket triage, documentation, monitoring, asset register maintenance, user onboarding/offboarding and backup-check reporting.
MSP offshoring exists, but the WA SME MSP market is not controlled by one player. Position around disciplined support rather than cheap helpdesk.
10
Agribusiness, food producers and regional exporters
Export documentation support, logistics admin, supplier and customer follow-up, bookkeeping support, inventory admin, seasonal labour admin and digital marketing.
Fragmented and regionally dispersed. Target growers, processors, packers, fisheries, wineries and food brands with export or wholesale complexity.
Best three to prioritise first
Residential construction and specialist trades should be the first campaign. The pain is immediate, the buyer is usually accessible, and the offshore roles are concrete. The best entry product is a dedicated project administration, estimating support or scheduling assistant.
Allied health and NDIS providers should be the second campaign. The sector has strong demand, recurring administration and clear owner fatigue. The best entry product is a clinic administration assistant trained in intake, billing, reminders and document workflows.
Mining services and METS SMEs should be the third campaign. WA has a large mining economy, but the better offshore market sits around the suppliers rather than the mine owners. The best entry product is mobilisation, procurement, HSEQ document control or maintenance planning administration.
Industries not prioritised
Generic retail should be deprioritised because many tasks require physical presence, margins are tight and offshore administration is already well understood by e-commerce operators.
Hospitality should be treated cautiously. It has obvious labour pressure, but the most painful roles are on site. Offshore support can help with bookkeeping, rostering and marketing, but the market is price-sensitive.
Accounting firms are receptive to offshoring, but the niche is increasingly mature. It can still work with a sharp proposition such as SMSF administration, bookkeeping overflow, payroll processing or client onboarding rather than broad offshore accounting.
Large mining, major banks, national insurers, universities and government agencies are poor early targets. Procurement is slow, compliance barriers are high and existing vendors are entrenched.

3. Outreach email and call script query and response
User query
