Instant Expert
A five-minute brief so you can talk to a residential building & trades prospect like someone who understands their world — not a
caller reading a script.
THE LABOUR MARKET THEY LIVE IN
WA residential building and specialist trades have ridden a long demand wave straight into a persistent skills shortage. Competition for
licensed tradespeople, supervisors and estimators is intense, and the same shortage extends to the office: good construction
administrators, contracts and scheduling staff are hard to find, slow to recruit and expensive to keep.
WHY THE ADMIN LOAD HURTS
Building is a high-transaction, document-heavy business. A single job generates quotes, take-offs, purchase orders, supplier follow-
ups, variations, RFIs, compliance paperwork, progress claims, invoices and constant client updates. In a 10–80 person firm there is
rarely a deep back office, so it lands on owners, supervisors, estimators and project managers doing admin at night — time not spent
winning or pricing work.
THE PRESSURES SHAPING THEIR YEAR
Margins are tight. Years of materials and labour cost inflation, fixed-price contracts signed before costs rose, and well-publicised
construction insolvencies have made owners cost-conscious and cautious about fixed overhead. Slow quote turnaround loses jobs,
poor supplier follow-up delays sites, and weak invoicing strangles cash flow.
WHERE OFFSHORE SUPPORT FITS
The credible message is separation of work, not replacement of people. Licensed, on-site and client-facing work stays local; the
repeatable back-office process work can be handled by a dedicated, trained, supervised offshore team member at a fraction of a local
hire — capacity and cash-flow relief, not cost-cutting.
TALK LIKE AN INSIDER
Know the language: take-offs, variations, RFIs, progress claims, retentions, defects, practical completion, PC/PS items. Know the tools:
Buildxact, Databuild, Simpro, ServiceM8, procore, Xero, MYOB. Listen first and name the one workflow that clearly hurts most.

Objection handling — residential building & trades
How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation
open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting for the Commercial Manager, not winning the point on the call.
Objection 	Response angle
“My jobs are all on-site.” 	Absolutely — and we'd never touch the on-site work. What we support is the office trail
behind it: quotes, variations, supplier follow-up, scheduling and invoicing. Which of those
eats the most of your team's week?
“I tried a VA and it failed.” 	That's common, and usually it's a scoping and management problem, not the person.
Brainbox is a managed model — we recruit, train, supervise and cover for the role, working
inside your systems. Worth seeing how that differs?
“Quoting is too technical to hand off.” 	The pricing judgement stays with your estimator — always. What an offshore assistant does
is the take-offs, supplier pricing chase, data entry and quote formatting so the estimator
prices faster. Would faster quotes win more work?
“Cash flow's tight right now.” 	Understood — which is often the reason to look. Chasing invoices and job-costing is exactly
the work an offshore role tightens, and it costs a fraction of a local admin hire. Happy to
compare the numbers.
“We're too small for this.” 	Many of our building clients are 15–40 people. One part-time offshore role removes the
admin an owner or supervisor is doing after hours — without building a bigger local team.
“Just send me information.” 	Happy to — it may be more useful to first know whether estimating, scheduling or invoicing
is the bigger pressure, so I send the right example. Which one is it?
Know when to stop. A clear "no" frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and
respect every opt-out immediately.

The reason we are speaking with builders and trade contractors is that the pressure is not only on site. It is also in quoting, take-offs,
supplier follow-up, scheduling, variation tracking, job admin, invoice chasing and client updates. Many businesses in the sector do not

Follow-up sequence
First touch opens the door; the follow-ups win the meeting. Each adds a new angle — never resends the same message. Stop the
sequence on any reply, opt-out or "do not contact".
Touch 	Purpose
1 · Day 1 	First-touch email + Industry Insight Article (previous page).
2 · Day 2–3 	Phone call using the script; LinkedIn connection request if not already connected.
3 · Day 5 	Value follow-up email — one specific angle from the proposition page.
4 · Day 9 	Second call + short LinkedIn message with a relevant proof point.
5 · Day 14 	Close-the-loop / breakup email — polite, no pressure, easy to reopen.
Day 45+ 	Move to nurture; revisit on a new trigger (hiring, growth, new work).
TOUCH 3 · VALUE FOLLOW-UP EMAIL
Hi [First Name], following my note on offshore support for builders — the pressure point most WA firms raise first is quote turnaround.
Getting take-offs, supplier pricing and quote formatting off the estimator's desk usually gets quotes out days faster. Would a short call
with our Commercial Manager be worth 20 minutes to see if it fits [Company]?
TOUCH 5 · CLOSE-THE-LOOP / BREAKUP EMAIL
Hi [First Name], I don't want to keep landing in your inbox. If offshore admin, estimating or scheduling support isn't a priority right now,
no problem at all — I'll close the loop. If it's just bad timing, tell me when to circle back and I will. Either way, the Industry Insight Article
is yours to keep.
Multi-thread, don't spam. Space the sequence across the month and vary who you contact within an account — never several identical
messages to one firm in a day.
