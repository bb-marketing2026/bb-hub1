MANAGERIAL ACCOUNTABILITY
38
Managerial accountability is critical in the pursuit of
business excellence because it ensures that leaders are
responsible for driving performance, aligning efforts with
organisational goals, and fostering a culture of continuous
improvement. Accountability establishes clarity in roles
and expectations, promoting transparency and trust while
enabling timely decision-making and corrective actions. Key
Result Areas (KRAs) that are divided into Business As Usual
(BAU) and Business Excellence (BE) play a foundational role
by balancing the dual focus on maintaining operational
stability and pursuing strategic improvements. BAU KRAs
ensure that essential processes are executed effectively,
meeting day-to-day performance benchmarks, while BE
KRAs challenge managers to innovate, optimise, and elevate
the organisation's capabilities. This structured approach
provides a comprehensive framework for managers to
deliver consistent results while strategically advancing the
organisation towards its vision of excellence.
12.1 Business As Usual
Business As Usual (BAU) management activities focus
on maintaining the ongoing operations and day-to-day
functions of an organisation. These activities ensure that
core processes, services, and deliverables are executed
efficiently, consistently, and in line with established standards
or customer expectations. BAU encompasses tasks such as
resource allocation, performance monitoring, compliance
with policies and regulations, and addressing routine
challenges that arise in the normal course of business.
The primary objective of BAU management is to sustain
operational stability, meet service level agreements, and
deliver predictable outcomes that support the organisation's
strategic goals. The outcomes and results specified in this
table largely reflect the individual efforts of the management
team, and a substantial proportion of management KPIs
and bonus structures will be derived from delivering these

12.2 Business Excellence
Business Excellence (BE) management activities are aimed at enhancing the organisation's processes, systems, and overall
performance to drive growth, efficiency, and innovation. These activities focus on identifying inefficiencies, implementing new
strategies, and adopting technologies or methodologies that improve quality, reduce costs, or increase customer satisfaction.
BI involves data analysis, cross-functional collaboration, and the application of frameworks like the management competency
framework to deliver measurable improvements. Unlike BAU, BI management is forward-looking, seeking to challenge the
status quo and position the organisation to adapt to market changes, seize opportunities, and achieve long-term success. The
outcomes and results specified in this table largely reflect the teamwork and joint efforts of the management team towards
