Role purpose & primary objective
The Lead Generation Specialist builds a consistent pipeline of qualified sales appointments for Brainbox. The focus is
Western Australian SME companies with 10–100 employees, plus selected Australian and US prospects with a clear fit
for offshore services.
WHY THIS ROLE MATTERS
The Lead Generation Specialist is the bridge between marketing and sales — turning marketing’s awareness and interest into
qualified, sales-ready conversations. The role is considered a key driver of new revenue for Brainbox: the pipeline it creates is
where future commercial transactions begin.
The primary output is a booked sales appointment with a suitable decision maker or senior influencer, recorded in
HubSpot with a clear business reason, contact history, company profile and recommended sales angle. The target is
quality-adjusted, not activity-only — the correct benchmark is appointments held, not appointments booked.

What counts as a qualified appointment
A meeting counts only when all of the following are met:
The company fits the Brainbox ideal customer profile.
The contact is a decision maker, budget holder,
operational owner or credible internal sponsor.
The prospect has at least one confirmed or strongly
evidenced business driver.
The meeting is accepted in the sales team calendar.
The HubSpot record contains clear context for the
sales person.
The prospect was not booked through misleading
claims, excessive pressure or vague bait.
Business drivers include: recruitment difficulty, cost pressure, growth, administration overload, a vacant role, delayed work,
process failure, poor software utilisation, need for extended hours, need for back-office support, or frustration with local hiring.
Qualify before you book — without over-qualifying
Use only the questions you need. Over-qualifying loses the meeting.
Which role or function is creating the most pressure
right now?
Is this mainly cost, capacity, recruitment difficulty or
process backlog?
Are you the right person, or someone who manages
operations or finance?
Have you used offshore or outsourced support before?
Would you prefer to discuss admin, finance, customer
service, marketing or operations support?

Sales-executive LinkedIn network growth
Brainbox sales conversations land better when the request comes from the sales executive’s own network. The Lead
Generation Specialist is responsible for growing that network on the executive’s behalf.
MANDATORY WEEKLY REQUIREMENT
Each week, the Lead Generation Specialist must log in as one of the sales executives (using approved, delegated access) and
generate at least 20 new, relevant connections from that executive’s LinkedIn profile — targeting decision makers and
influencers that match the Brainbox ICP and the week’s vertical focus.
HOW TO RUN IT
Use only delegated access the sales executive has
explicitly approved — never undisclosed or shared
credentials outside that arrangement.
Send personalised connection requests as the
executive, matched to the weekly vertical and a
relevant trigger.
Keep the executive’s voice and tone — warm, human,
no pitch in the connection note.
Stagger requests through the week to stay within
LinkedIn limits and look human.
Record each connected contact in HubSpot
with source “Sales-exec LinkedIn” and the
owning executive.
STANDARDS & GUARDRAILS
Minimum 20 new accepted-or-pending relevant
connection requests per executive profile, per
week; quality and fit come before raw volume.
No automation tools that breach LinkedIn rules or risk
the executive’s account.
Respect every opt-out, decline and “do not contact”
signal immediately.
Hand newly connected contacts into the normal
nurture and outreach cadence.
Report new connections per executive in the
weekly report.
EXAMPLE CONNECTION NOTE (SENT AS THE EXECUTIVE)
“Hi [Name], I work with WA SMEs on building high-performing teams and managed offshore support. I noticed your team is
active in [sector] — it would be good to connect.”

Marketing-led campaign tasks
As the bridge between marketing and sales, the Lead Generation Specialist also delivers campaign-specific tasks
allocated by the Marketing Manager — event and webinar follow-ups, content and EDM lists, sector campaigns and
inbound enquiry qualification.
HOW CAMPAIGN WORK FITS THE WEEK
Campaign tasks are not run separately from the core cadence — they must be cross-referenced into the existing weekly
activities and targets. Campaign follow-ups count toward first-touch, follow-up, call and conversation targets; campaign-
sourced meetings count toward booked and held appointments.

upcoming campaigns and the specific tasks allocated.

Marketing Manager and in the weekly report.

Day-by-day cadence
Monday — plan & build
Monday creates control of the week: define who will be targeted, why they are likely to care, and what appointment
outcome is expected.
Block 	Action
8:30–9:00 	Sales alignment. Review last week’s booked & held meetings, capture sales feedback on lead
quality, agree the weekly sector focus, message theme, appointment capacity and the case
studies / offers to use.
9:00–10:30 	Target account build. Build / refresh 20–30 priority accounts via Sales Navigator, Apollo, HubSpot,
job ads, directories and referral lists. Filter on WA, 10–100 staff, the weekly vertical, private
ownership, stable/positive headcount, recent hiring and visible support functions.
10:30–11:30 	Account scoring (see model below). Tier 1 (80+) gets multi-threaded outreach this week; Tier 2
(60–79) enters nurture with selected direct outreach; Tier 3 (40–59) is held for a trigger.
11:30–12:30 	Buyer mapping. Identify 3–5 contacts per Tier 1 account — at least one senior decision maker and
one operational influencer. Record role, LinkedIn, email, phone, source, confidence, buying
influence and proposed angle.
1:00–2:30 	Trigger research. Find the specific reason to contact each priority account (hiring, growth,
capacity, cost, process, leadership, service or content trigger). Record the exact trigger — never
generic assumptions.
2:30–3:30 	Message prep. Create a personalised angle per Tier 1 account and three weekly message
variations (Owner/MD, Operations/GM, Finance/HR). Connect to a business issue, not a generic
outsourcing pitch.
3:30–4:30 	HubSpot set-up. Create tasks & sequences, account notes, lifecycle stage, lead status, next-
activity date, vertical & pain tags, owner; check for duplicates and prior “do not contact” status.
4:30–5:00 	Close-out. Send the sales lead a summary: vertical focus, Tier 1 count, contacts mapped, best
five accounts to watch, main triggers, planned Tuesday first-touch volume, and any data / tool
issues.
Hints & tips. Build the list before writing a single message — targeting beats volume. Bank three reusable angles per vertical so
Tuesday starts fast. If an account has no clear trigger, it is not a Monday priority.

Objection handling
How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the
conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting, not winning the point.
Objection 	Response angle
“We are too small.” 	That is often why this works — one offshore role removes repeatable work without building
a larger local management structure.
“We tried outsourcing before.” 	Useful context. Brainbox is a managed offshore staffing model, not a task marketplace —
the difference is recruitment, supervision, process design and retention.
“We only hire locally.” 	That makes sense for client-facing or site-based work. Brainbox usually supports the
process work behind local staff, not the local relationship roles.
“We do not have budget.” 	The starting point is comparing current local hiring cost, overtime, delays and
management load against an offshore support role.
“Send me information.” 	Happy to send a short summary — it may be more useful to first understand whether
you’re looking at admin, finance, customer service, marketing or operations support.
“Not now.” 	Understood. Is this worth revisiting in three months, or should I close the loop?
“We’re happy as we are.” 	Great to hear — most of our clients were too. They added support to protect that, not to fix
a problem. Worth a 15-minute compare?
“What about data security?” 	Fair question. Brainbox staff work under managed access, supervision and clear policies
— happy to walk your team through exactly how that works.
“We’ll sort it internally.” 	Understandable. It usually comes down to timing and management load — offshore
support often bridges the gap until that internal hire lands.
Call angles by persona
OWNER / MD
“Many WA owners are not trying to replace local staff —
they are trying to remove the process work that stops local
staff selling, servicing clients or managing projects.”
OPERATIONS
“Brainbox can support the repeatable work that builds up
behind scheduling, quoting, customer updates, inboxes,
reconciliations and CRM administration.”
FINANCE
“Brainbox can help compare the loaded cost of local
support roles against managed offshore staff for process-
heavy work.”
HR
“Brainbox can help where a role is hard to fill locally, has
high turnover, or needs stable support without running
another recruitment process.”
Know when to stop. A clear “no” is a gift — it frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if
appropriate, and move on. Respect every opt-out immediately.

Your toolkit & how it fits together
Five tools carry the role. Each has a clear job — the value is in the handoff between them: discover, enrich, draft, record,
send, then record again.
LINKEDIN & SALES NAVIGATOR
Discover and research accounts and decision makers, watch
for triggers and warm paths, engage socially, and grow the
sales executives’ networks (20+ new connections each week).
APOLLO
Find, filter, enrich and validate contacts — verified emails,
direct dials and current titles — then push only action-ready
contacts to HubSpot. Review bounces daily; never dump
large lists.
CLAUDE
Synthesise account research, draft personalised first-touch
and follow-up variations, summarise prospects and build clean
handover notes — faster output that keeps the warm, kind
Brainbox tone. Always review before sending.
HUBSPOT
The source of truth. Every account, contact, task, sequence,
lifecycle stage and outcome lives here, with the reporting and
dashboards that track pipeline through to revenue.
OUTLOOK
Compliant email outreach and the calendar — send sender-identified email, book discovery meetings directly into the sales team’s
calendars, and issue invites with the agenda and video link.
The interplay — one flow
1 · LinkedIn / Sales Navigator — discover & trigger 	2 · Apollo — enrich & validate 	3 · Claude — research & draft
4 · HubSpot — record, task & sequence 	5 · Outlook — send & book 	6 · HubSpot — log, report & hand over
HubSpot sits at the centre: Apollo and LinkedIn feed clean records in, Claude shapes the message, Outlook executes the touch and
the booking, and every result returns to HubSpot — so nothing is lost between marketing, the specialist and sales.
Golden rules
One system of record. If it isn’t in HubSpot, it
didn’t happen.
Enrich before you send. Apollo confidence checked;
no guessed personal emails.
Claude drafts, you decide. Always review and
personalise before sending.
LinkedIn stays human. No automation that risks an
account or the brand.
Outlook sends, HubSpot logs. Every email and
meeting tracked or logged.
Close the loop daily. Bounces, opt-outs and replies
cleared before new work.
