Why this industry

The pressure

Dispatch, proof of delivery, customer updates, invoicing, compliance records, fleet maintenance, driver documentation and after-hours communication all need to be handled quickly and accurately — but much of it does not need to be done by local operational staff.

The opportunity

Offshore support strengthens the back office around dispatch teams, fleet managers, compliance officers and accounts staff — POD processing, customer updates, compliance registers, invoice support, fleet maintenance admin, fatigue documentation and job data entry — so local staff stay focused on drivers, customers and vehicles.

Market shapeFragmented outside the major carriers. Target regional freight, specialised logistics, courier networks, cold chain, machinery transport and mining-supply logistics operators.

Instant Expert

A five-minute brief so you can talk to a transport & logistics prospect like someone who understands their world — not a caller reading a script.

The labour market they live in

Transport competes for drivers first, but the office is just as stretched — allocators, dispatch, compliance and accounts roles are hard to fill and easy to lose. WA's distances and 24/7 operating windows mean the admin never really stops, and after-hours coverage is a constant headache for lean teams.

Why the admin load hurts

Every load generates dispatch instructions, proof-of-delivery capture, customer updates, compliance and fatigue records, invoicing and job data entry. When this backs up, invoices go out late, customers chase updates, compliance registers fall behind and operational staff are pulled off moving freight to fix paperwork.

The pressures shaping their year

Margins are thin and fuel, wages and compliance costs keep rising. Chain-of-responsibility and fatigue obligations are unforgiving, and late invoicing hurts cash flow. Customers expect real-time updates that a busy allocator can't always give — so service and cash both suffer.

Where offshore support fits

Drivers, vehicles and on-road decisions stay local. A dedicated offshore resource handles the structured back office — POD processing, customer updates, compliance and fatigue records, invoicing and data entry — often extending useful coverage outside standard Perth office hours.

Talk like an insider

Know the language: allocation, PODs, consignment notes, chain of responsibility, NHVAS, fatigue management, mass/dimension, 3PL, cold chain, linehaul. Know the tools: MyTrucking, TransVirtual, CargoWise, M&L, telematics. Speak to responsiveness, compliance and cash flow.

Objection handling — transport & logistics

How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting for the Commercial Manager, not winning the point on the call.
ObjectionResponse angle
“It all has to happen here.”The on-road work does — but POD processing, customer updates, compliance records and invoicing don't. Which of those backs up most in your week?
“Compliance is too critical.”Exactly why consistency helps. A trained resource keeps your fatigue and CoR registers current — you keep ownership of the obligations.
“We run 24/7.”That's a strength here — offshore hours can extend your useful coverage beyond a local 9–5 without paying penalty rates.
“Tried outsourcing, didn't work.”Usually scoping and supervision. Our managed model trains the person in your systems and covers leave — not a gig marketplace.
“Margins are tight.”Which is the reason to look — faster invoicing and less overtime admin at a fraction of a local hire directly helps cash flow.
“Just send info.”Happy to — is it POD, customer updates or invoicing that hurts most? I'll send the right example.
Know when to stop. A clear "no" frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and respect every opt-out immediately.
