1.1 · Purpose of this playbook

The shared picture — the purpose of the function, the systems and controls that govern every transaction, the delegations that bound it, and the cycles that keep it compliant and predictable.

This playbook sets a clear, consistent framework for managing the business’s finances, meeting statutory obligations and Philippine Accounting Standards, and removes ambiguity from how finance work is done.

Finance work fails when it relies on memory or on any one person holding the whole process. This playbook replaces that with structure: defined systems, separated duties, bounded authorities and a predictable calendar — so the numbers are accurate, the controls hold, and nothing compliance-critical is ever a surprise.

Who it applies to

The whole finance function — the GM – Corporate, the Accountant and the Accounting Assistant — and anyone who logs into the financial systems or exercises a financial delegation.

When you may deviate

Only where a Director authorises it, or where statutory compliance requires it. Controls and separation of duties are never bypassed for convenience; every exception is documented and reviewed.

The aimMake finance predictable, auditable and trusted — so good financial outcomes don’t depend on any one person remembering the right thing at the right moment. No changes without a Director’s consent; reviewed at least annually and re-issued, never amended on the run.

1.4 · Financial controls & separation of duties

Separation of duties is the principle that no single person controls an end-to-end process — a system of checks and balances giving process integrity with proper security and quality control.

Operation vs record

The person who acts is not the only person who records.

Admin vs audit

Separate system administration from auditing, and ownership from certification — so review stays independent.

Prepare vs approve

The person who prepares a payment is not the person who approves it. Authorisation follows the delegations (1.5).

How a payment splits across the team

Prepare
Assistant / Accountant

Draft the bill in Xero, attach the receipt and supporting documents, & apply appropriate GL coding.

Review & approve bill
Accountant

Check validity, accuracy, supplier details, coding and completeness of supporting documents. Once satisfied, approve the bill in Xero.

Prepare payment
Assistant / Accountant

Following approval of the bill in Xero, prepare the corresponding payment in BPI or the applicable banking platform and submit it for authorisation. The payment preparer cannot authorise or release their own payment.

Authorise payment
GM (per delegation)

Review and approve/release the payment in the banking platform where the expenditure is within the approved budget and the GM’s delegated authority. Payments outside the approved budget or delegated authority must be escalated in accordance with 1.5.

Reconcile
Independent reconciler

Reconcile the bank back to Xero.

Reviewed, not assumedSeparation of duties is reviewed at least annually and tested as part of the May separation-of-duties audit, to ensure appropriate, proportionate controls apply to every key financial system and that incompatible responsibilities have not accumulated with a single individual.

1.5 · Delegations & authorities

The Board authorises others to exercise defined powers within limits. Authority is vested in a position, not a person, and the Board remains ultimately accountable. The authoritative source is the Schedule of Management Authorities; key Finance lines are in 4.2.

  1. Exercise authority in line with the law, company policy and any conditions on the delegation — for a proper business purpose, with no conflict of interest.
  2. Never approve a transaction you personally benefit from, and never approve beyond your delegated limit — doing so breaches policy and may constitute misconduct.
  3. A procurement authority must hold a financial delegation that meets or exceeds the contract’s aggregate annual value.

Board

Approves the largest commitments, budgets, investments, auditor and policy. Ultimately accountable.

GM – Corporate

Approves operating expenditure and contracts within limits; recommends above them; signs banking.

Accountant

Develops, prepares and actions — executes approved payments and prepares data; does not self-approve.

The action verbsEach action is one of Develop · Recommend · Approve · Be advised · Ratify · Action — defined in 4.2. When in doubt about your limit, treat it as beyond your authority and escalate.

1.6 · Confidentiality & information security

Finance handles the most sensitive information in the business — payroll, bank access, director and commercial data. Protecting it is foundational, not optional. Read this section as binding.

The absolute rulePayroll, banking and all personal data relating to staff and the directors is highly confidential and is not shared with any other party — including those who claim a “right to know”.

Credentials & access

Strong, unique passwords and MFA on Xero, BPI, Metrobank and email. Credentials never shared; banking authorisation stays with the authorised person only.

Screen & documents

Lock screens when away; keep payroll and bank data off personal devices; store records only in Xero and OneDrive. Clean desk; no sensitive print-outs left out.

Fraud & payment safety

Verify any change to supplier or staff bank details independently before paying. Be alert to invoice and email fraud; when a request feels off, pause and confirm through a known channel.

Serious misconductUnauthorised disclosure will be treated as Serious Misconduct. If anyone presses for salary, payment or personal financial information, the answer is a polite decline and escalation to the GM or a Director. If unsure whether something may be shared, say nothing and ask a Director first.

1.7 · Operating philosophy & escalation

A few principles sit under every finance decision. They explain why the controls and cycles are shaped the way they are — and how an issue should move.

Accuracy first

The books are right before they are fast. Reconcile, check and support every figure; correct the system before proceeding.

Controls hold

Separation of duties and delegations are never bypassed for speed. A control skipped once becomes a control gone.

Compliance on time

Statutory dates are immovable. The calendar is worked ahead of time, never caught up to after a deadline.

How an issue moves

Step 1
Spotted

A discrepancy, error or risk is noticed.

Step 2
Logged

Captured in Xero notes or the Asana audit project.

Step 3
Assessed

Accountant judges materiality & authority.

Step 4
Resolved / raised

Fixed within authority, or escalated to GM & Board.

Step 5
Closed

Closed on confirmation; root cause addressed.

Escalate earlyAnything beyond delegated authority, anything material, or anything touching fraud or a Director’s finances is raised at once — with the facts and the options, not just a question.

1.11 · Non-negotiable rules & outcome

  1. Xero is the sole source of truth — everything reconciles to it; accounts change only with the GM’s approval.
  2. Separation of duties holds — no one person prepares, approves and reconciles the same payment.
  3. No one approves beyond their delegated limit, or where they personally benefit.
  4. Statutory dates are never missed — the compliance calendar is worked ahead of every deadline.
  5. Payroll, banking and personal data are confidential — unauthorised disclosure is Serious Misconduct.
  6. The Finance team reviews this playbook every quarter and gives the Board assurance; it changes only with a Director’s consent and is re-issued.
Operating outcomeWhen it is followed, the numbers are accurate, the controls hold, compliance is never late, and the Board can rely on what it sees. Predictable finance builds trust — and trust is what lets the business move with confidence.

Part Four · Reference

4.4 · Significant accounting policies

A plain-language summary of the policies that govern the books. The authoritative wording lives in the Finance Operations Manual; these are the working essentials.

Basis of preparation

General-purpose statements under Philippine Accounting Standards, on the accrual basis and historical cost — modified where applicable by fair value.

VAT

Revenue, expenses and assets are net of VAT except where not recoverable from the BIR. Net VAT due to/from the BIR sits in payables/receivables.

Fixed assets & capitalisation

Carried at cost (or fair value) less depreciation. Equipment under the capitalisation threshold (~$1,000) is expensed and held on an asset inventory listing.

Depreciation

Straight-line over useful life from the date ready for use; rates, residuals and useful lives reviewed each reporting period.

Receivables & payables

Receivables reviewed for collectability; uncollectible debts written off when identified. Trade payables unsecured and normally paid within 30 days.

Employee benefits & leases

Benefits provided for as services are rendered. Finance leases capitalised; operating-lease payments expensed over the term.

Current vs non-current is judged on the 12-month operating cycle. Figures rounded to the nearest dollar; the chart of accounts changes only with the GM’s approval.

5.2 · Australian BAS, PAYG, FBT & company tax

Four ATO obligations, the same four-step shape — gather & reconcile, prepare, review & approve, then lodge & pay. Most are lodged through the tax/BAS agent, which extends the due dates shown.

ObligationPrepareLodge & pay
BAS · GSTReconcile GST & bank in Xero; prepare BAS (GST, PAYG-W & instalment)Lodge & pay ATO · 28th after period
PAYGPAYG-W from STP; confirm instalment rate; carried onto the BASPaid with the BAS
FBTIdentify fringe benefits; gross-up & calculate taxable value; GM & tax agent reviewLodge & pay · 21 May (later via agent)
Company taxFinalise year-end accounts; tax reconciliation & adjustments; prepare CTR with tax agent; Board reviewLodge & pay/refund · agent due date
NoteDue dates assume lodgement through the registered tax/BAS agent, which generally extends them — confirm each period against the ATO lodgement program. GST & PAYG-W reconcile back to Xero and STP before lodging.

5.3 · Philippine compliance

Every PH lodgement follows the same five-step shape. Three streams run on it — statutory contributions, tax, and the annual filings. Dates are immovable; the calendar (1.9) drives the month.

1
Gather

Pull payroll, sales & purchase data from Xero and the payroll workbook.

2
Compute & prepare

Compute amounts due; prepare the BIR form or contribution schedule.

3
Review & approve

GM reviews & approves; deductions reconciled to remittances.

4
File & remit

eFPS/eBIR for BIR, EPRS for PhilHealth, accredited banks / Bayad Centers.

5
File proof

Save confirmations & ORs to OneDrive; record & reconcile in Xero.

5.4 · Philippine payroll

The fortnightly run for Brainsource — built in the payroll workbook, finalised in Xero, paid through BPI, then remitted to the agencies. Preparer is not the approver.

1
Prepare

Duplicate latest sheet; update from Masterlist; extract leave from BambooHR.

2
Compute

Plot leave & holiday premiums (100%/30%); calculate gross, deductions, net.

3
Payslips & approve

Build pay run in Xero; GM approves; issue payslips.

4
Pay (BPI)

Verify bank-detail changes; import recipients’ CSV; submit batch.

5
Remit & reconcile

Remit SSS, PhilHealth, HDMF & withholding tax; reconcile in Xero.

Cadence & controlFortnightly on Wednesdays. PH regular-holiday premium 100%, special non-working 30% (ordinary days unless also a WA holiday). Preparer ≠ approver; bank-detail changes verified; payroll data strictly confidential.
