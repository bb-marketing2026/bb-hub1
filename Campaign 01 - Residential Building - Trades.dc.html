Why this industry

The pressure

Quoting, take-offs, supplier follow-up, project scheduling, variation tracking, invoice chasing and client updates all compete with the work that actually protects margin on site. Owners, supervisors, estimators and project managers absorb this admin themselves because another senior local hire is slow and expensive.

The best entry product is a dedicated project administration, estimating support or scheduling assistant.

Instant Expert

A five-minute brief so you can talk to a residential building & trades prospect like someone who understands their world — not a caller reading a script.

The labour market they live in

WA residential building and specialist trades have ridden a long demand wave straight into a persistent skills shortage. Competition for licensed tradespeople, supervisors and estimators is intense, and the same shortage extends to the office: good construction administrators, contracts and scheduling staff are hard to find, slow to recruit and expensive to keep.

Why the admin load hurts

Building is a high-transaction, document-heavy business. A single job generates quotes, take-offs, purchase orders, supplier follow-ups, variations, RFIs, compliance paperwork, progress claims, invoices and constant client updates. In a 10–80 person firm there is rarely a deep back office, so it lands on owners, supervisors, estimators and project managers doing admin at night — time not spent winning or pricing work.

The pressures shaping their year

Margins are tight. Years of materials and labour cost inflation, fixed-price contracts signed before costs rose, and well-publicised construction insolvencies have made owners cost-conscious and cautious about fixed overhead. Slow quote turnaround loses jobs, poor supplier follow-up delays sites, and weak invoicing strangles cash flow.

Where offshore support fits

The credible message is separation of work, not replacement of people. Licensed, on-site and client-facing work stays local; the repeatable back-office process work can be handled by a dedicated, trained, supervised offshore team member at a fraction of a local hire — capacity and cash-flow relief, not cost-cutting.

Talk like an insider

Know the language: take-offs, variations, RFIs, progress claims, retentions, defects, practical completion, PC/PS items. Know the tools: Buildxact, Databuild, Simpro, ServiceM8, procore, Xero, MYOB. Listen first and name the one workflow that clearly hurts most.

Objection handling — residential building & trades

How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting for the Commercial Manager, not winning the point on the call.
ObjectionResponse angle
“My jobs are all on-site.”Absolutely — and we'd never touch the on-site work. What we support is the office trail behind it: quotes, variations, supplier follow-up, scheduling and invoicing. Which of those eats the most of your team's week?
“I tried a VA and it failed.”That's common, and usually it's a scoping and management problem, not the person. Brainbox is a managed model — we recruit, train, supervise and cover for the role, working inside your systems. Worth seeing how that differs?
“Quoting is too technical to hand off.”The pricing judgement stays with your estimator — always. What an offshore assistant does is the take-offs, supplier pricing chase, data entry and quote formatting so the estimator prices faster. Would faster quotes win more work?
“Cash flow's tight right now.”Understood — which is often the reason to look. Chasing invoices and job-costing is exactly the work an offshore role tightens, and it costs a fraction of a local admin hire. Happy to compare the numbers.
“We're too small for this.”Many of our building clients are 15–40 people. One part-time offshore role removes the admin an owner or supervisor is doing after hours — without building a bigger local team.
“Just send me information.”Happy to — it may be more useful to first know whether estimating, scheduling or invoicing is the bigger pressure, so I send the right example. Which one is it?
Know when to stop. A clear "no" frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and respect every opt-out immediately.
