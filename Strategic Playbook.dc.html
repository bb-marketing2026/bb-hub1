Section 1 · Orientation

Purpose & how to read this playbook

This Strategic Playbook sets out where Brainbox is going over the next four years and how the business is organised to get there. It is the parent document of the Brainbox playbook suite: the Revenue, Operations, Finance, Facilities and Governance playbooks — and every subsidiary beneath them — execute a defined part of this plan. Where this document sets direction, the functional playbooks hold the operational detail.

Brainbox is a Philippines-based business process outsourcing partner to Australian organisations. We do not run a call centre; we place specialised, tailored offshore talent — from academic assessors and engineers to allied-health administrators and back-office professionals — alongside Australian teams. Our single corporate value, “Always Be Kind,” underwrites every decision in this plan.

How to read this documentSections 1–6 establish who we are, how we are organised, and the market we operate in. Sections 7–14 set out the strategy, the money and the implementation. Sections 15–16 define how the Executive Leadership Team is held accountable for delivering it, through Business-As-Usual and Business-Excellence key result areas.

The plan in one lineGrow the billable workforce to 120 staff (100 by FY27, 120 by FY28), open the United States market, diversify how clients engage us, and make the new Brainbox building work harder — all while holding a profit margin of 20% and achieving ISO 27001 accreditation.

Section 3 · Starting point

Brainbox at the start of FY25

A clear, honest read of where the business stands today — its people, its offering, its clients and its assets — is the foundation every strategic choice is built on.

The workforce

Brainbox employs 58 people — 41 billable and 17 non-billable. The billable team delivers specialised offshore roles; the non-billable team runs the business that supports them.

Service offerings

Academic assessors, engineers, allied-health and medical administration, and traditional back-office support (bookkeeping, payroll, data and admin). The breadth lets us tailor to each client rather than sell a single product.

Clients & geography

Our clients are Australian businesses, concentrated in Western Australia. That focus built a reputation for reliability — and a concentration risk this plan is designed to reduce.

The new Brainbox building

A modern, purpose-built facility with room to grow, strong technology infrastructure, and sub-leasable space that can offset cost and seed partnerships.

Our unique selling propositionSpecialisation (roles most BPOs don’t offer), customisation (solutions built to each client), value alignment (“Always Be Kind” in every interaction) and operational efficiency (a lean structure and modern facility) — together these let us command premium positioning in a crowded market.

Section 6 · SWOT — Strengths

Strengths — what we can press

Brainbox competes from a genuinely differentiated base. These are the assets the strategy leans on, and the ones we must protect as we scale.

Specialisation few BPOs match

We place academic assessors, engineers, allied-health and medical administrators and specialised back-office professionals — not undifferentiated call-centre seats. This lets us compete on capability and outcome rather than price alone, and to command premium positioning in niches most competitors avoid.

Tailored, consultative delivery

Every engagement is built to the client’s need rather than sold from a fixed menu. That flexibility deepens relationships, lifts retention, and creates natural expansion paths from a single role into a standing team.

A values-led culture that clients feel

“Always Be Kind” is not a slogan on a wall — it shapes how our people work with client teams. It is a real driver of retention on both sides of the relationship and a genuine point of difference in a commoditised market.

A lean, accountable structure

Five clear Divisions and a seven-person Executive Leadership Team keep decisions fast and ownership unambiguous. As we grow toward 100 billable staff, this structure scales without adding management drag.

A modern, expandable facility

The new Brainbox building carries the current team, has room to grow, offers strong technology infrastructure for dual shifts, and holds sub-leasable space that can offset cost and seed partnerships.

An established reputation in WA

Years of reliable delivery to Western Australian clients give us reference-able credibility — the testimonials and case studies that de-risk entry into new sectors and the US market.

Strategic readOur strengths are real but concentrated in one market. The task is to carry them into new geographies and engagement models before the reputation that underpins them is diluted by fast growth.

Section 6 · SWOT — Weaknesses

Weaknesses — what we must close

An honest read of where Brainbox is exposed today. Each weakness maps directly to a strategic objective designed to close it.

Heavy reliance on one market

Our clients are almost entirely Australian and concentrated in Western Australia. A downturn, a policy shift or the loss of a few key accounts would hit revenue disproportionately. Geographic and client diversification is the single largest risk-reduction move in this plan.

A small, concentrated client base

Too much revenue sits with too few clients. Until the base broadens, pricing power and negotiating leverage sit with the client, and churn in any one account is material to the whole business.

Limited direct sales capability

Historically we have relied on the Australian Directors and referrals, leaving us without a repeatable, in-house engine for winning new work. Building direct sales capacity in Australia and the Philippines is a core FY25–FY28 initiative.

Margin below target

Profit sat at 15% in FY25 and tightened to 13% in FY26 as the business scaled — below the 20% target. Cost discipline and higher-margin work must move margin back toward plan even as headcount grows.

Accreditation not yet in place

Without ISO 27001 we are shut out of the most valuable regulated work in healthcare, education and technology. It is a weakness today and an unlocked opportunity the moment it is achieved.

Thin capability in new-market compliance

We do not yet hold the legal and compliance depth for US operations (HIPAA, FERPA, state privacy law). This must be built or bought before US delivery can scale safely.

Strategic readNone of these weaknesses is structural — each has a named objective and owner in this plan. The risk is sequencing: growing headcount and revenue faster than we close margin, sales capability and accreditation.

Section 6 · SWOT — Opportunities

Opportunities — what we can capture

The external tailwinds the strategy is built to catch. AI leads this list — it is the opportunity with the greatest potential to reshape our economics.

Artificial intelligence — the defining opportunity

AI lets a specialised BPO do far more with the same people. Applied to our delivery — drafting, research, summarisation, quality checking, data handling — it raises each billable person’s output and lets us take on work that was previously uneconomic. Applied to our own operations, it automates repetitive internal tasks and sharpens decision-making. Used well, AI lifts margin, widens the range of roles we can offer, and lets us price competitively while protecting profit. It is the single largest lever on the 20% margin target, and it is why an AI Transformation Playbook sits within the suite.

The US market

The world’s largest outsourcing market, mature in its use of offshore partners, with high demand in exactly our niches — healthcare, education, engineering and technology. Dual shifts let us serve it from assets we already own.

Flexible engagement models

Offering part-time and project-based work alongside full-time opens the door to startups, seasonal businesses and cautious first-time buyers — a far wider addressable market, and often at higher margins for short-cycle expertise.

Growing acceptance of offshore work

Remote and offshore delivery is now mainstream, reducing the friction and stigma that once slowed adoption. The market is more willing to buy what we sell than at any point in our history.

Sweating the building

Sub-leasing underutilised space offsets cost, and an innovation hub of complementary tenants can seed partnerships and even acquisition conversations — turning a fixed cost into a strategic asset.

Strategic readThese opportunities compound: AI raises capacity, dual shifts and flexible models widen the market that capacity can serve, and the building funds the expansion. The window is open now — the advantage goes to whoever moves first and governs it well.

Section 6 · SWOT — Threats

Threats — what we must blunt

The external risks that could derail the plan. AI appears here as well as in opportunities — the same force that lifts us can, left unmanaged, erode our value.

Artificial intelligence — the two-edged threat

The same technology that is our largest opportunity is also a direct threat. As AI automates routine knowledge work, some tasks clients once outsourced to us they may soon do themselves — commoditising the low-complexity end of our offering and compressing price. If competitors adopt AI faster, they undercut us on cost. And AI introduces new operational and compliance risk: data leakage into third-party models, inaccurate output reaching clients, and unclear regulatory treatment of automated handling of sensitive data. The mitigation is deliberate: move our people up the value chain into judgement-led, specialised work AI cannot replace, adopt AI faster than the market rather than slower, and govern its use tightly through the AI Transformation and Governance playbooks.

Economic volatility

Downturns in Australia or the US squeeze client budgets and demand. Our current market concentration makes this especially sharp — another reason diversification is urgent.

Regulatory complexity across geographies

Operating across the Philippines, Australia and the US multiplies compliance obligations — HIPAA, FERPA, the Australian Privacy Act and NDB scheme, and local labour law. A breach or lapse carries legal penalty and reputational damage.

Competition for talent

Scaling to 100 billable staff without diluting quality means winning skilled people in a competitive market. Wage pressure and attrition threaten both margin and delivery standards.

Cyber and reputational risk

As custodians of sensitive client data, a cybersecurity incident would damage trust and revenue at once. ISO 27001, a hardened security posture and a crisis plan are the direct answers.

Strategic readThe recurring theme is concentration and pace: too reliant on one market, and racing technology and competitors we do not control. Diversify faster than the threats mature, and adopt AI on our terms rather than having it done to us.

Section 7 · The strategy

The four-year growth thesis — six objectives

Six interconnected objectives drive growth, efficiency and market leadership. Each builds on Brainbox’s current strengths while addressing the risks named in the market analysis.

1

Expand the billable workforce to 120

6

Build a partner network to accelerate market penetration and keep a steady pipeline of opportunities flowing.

Section 8 · Spotlight — US Expansion

The US Expansion Project

The single largest growth and diversification move in the plan, and the one most dependent on the others landing first. It has its own project structure and is documented in full within the Revenue Playbook.

Why the US

The world’s largest, most outsourcing-mature market, with strong demand in our niches — healthcare, education, engineering and technology. The Philippines–US time gap lets us serve it on dual shifts from the same workstations, maximising assets we already own.

The target

5% of revenue from the US by FY27 and 10% by FY28; compliance frameworks in place by FY26; first US clients signed in FY27.

How we enter

Market research and segmentation; a US-focused website, content and referenceable Australian case studies; local sales agents and consulting partners to establish credibility; and a dedicated delivery team as volume builds.

What must be true first

Compliance depth (HIPAA, FERPA, state privacy law), an ISO 27001 posture, dual-shift operations, and enough billable capacity to staff US work without starving Australian delivery.

Status & ownershipOwned by the Revenue Division with Governance on compliance; run as a cross-functional project reporting to the Directors. As at FY26 the US contributes 0% of revenue — opening this market is the defining FY27 priority. Full detail lives in the Revenue Playbook’s US Expansion section.

Section 8 · Spotlight — AI Transformation

AI transformation — how it fits

The SWOT names AI as both our largest opportunity and a serious threat. This page sets the strategic stance; the sequenced, tool-by-tool transformation is documented in the AI Transformation Playbook.

The opportunity

AI lifts each billable person’s output, automates internal work, and lets us take on tasks that were previously uneconomic — the single largest lever on the 20% margin target and on the range of roles we can offer.

The threat

The same technology can commoditise low-complexity work, let faster competitors undercut us, and introduce data-leakage, accuracy and regulatory risk if adopted carelessly.

Our stance

  1. Move up the value chain. Shift our people toward judgement-led, specialised work AI cannot replace, and let AI carry the routine.
  2. Adopt faster than the market. Treat AI as a capability to build deliberately across every Division, not a tool to bolt on late.
  3. Govern it tightly. Control data handling, model use and output quality through the Governance Division’s framework.
Where the detail livesEach Division runs its own AI sub-journey. The staged 30/60/90-day rollouts, the tools, and the guardrails are documented in the AI Transformation Playbook, a companion to this document within the suite.

Section 15 · Accountability

Managerial accountability — BAU & Business Excellence

Managerial accountability makes leaders responsible for performance and aligns effort with the strategy. Brainbox splits Key Result Areas (KRAs) into two complementary halves.

Business As Usual (BAU)

Sustaining operations and day-to-day performance — resource allocation, performance monitoring, compliance and routine delivery. These outcomes are largely the individual accountability of each ELT member, and a substantial share of KPIs and bonuses derives from delivering them.

Business Excellence (BE)

Forward-looking improvement — challenging the status quo, adopting new methods and technology, and lifting the organisation’s capability. These outcomes reflect the joint teamwork of the ELT toward shared strategic goals.
