Purpose

This Revenue Playbook guides how Brainbox earns revenue. It brings the whole revenue function into one document, so the people who win and grow work operate from a shared plan. It is the parent of a set of playbooks that hold the operational detail: a Sales Playbook and a Marketing Playbook, each with its own subsets, plus a dedicated section for the US Expansion Project.

The document is organised as one fully documented section per layer, in the order revenue is actually earned. Each layer sets out what it owns, how it works, and its own AI transformation sub-journey, then closes with the defined interface by which it hands off to the next. Those seams are where a revenue engine either connects or leaks, so they are named, owned and measured.

How to read this documentEach layer begins on a new page with a banner showing what it receives and what it hands on. This playbook holds the model, the strategy and the plan for change; the operational detail lives in the child playbooks beneath it.

How revenue is earned

Brainbox earns revenue through five connected layers. The engine works when they connect; it stalls when any one runs on its own.

Converts those opportunities into signed engagements, through an Australian Commercial Manager and US Agents who work under Master Agents by territory.

Keeps and grows signed clients — expansion, retention and referrals — and feeds proof and warm leads back to the front of the engine.

The revenue model at Brainbox

Brainbox sells consultative offshoring and HR advisory to Australian SMEs and US general agencies. Buyers arrive through relationships, referrals, partnerships and direct conversations with people they trust — rarely through a blog post or a search result. The model concentrates effort on the human motions that produce work, and is measured against signed engagements, not raw enquiry counts.

The transformation thesis

Two shifts define the new capability layer.

Direct, don’t produce

AI now does most routine making across design, social, development and copy. A function of fewer people who direct AI systems outperforms more people operating tools by hand.

Reorient toward the sellers

Capability exists to make the sellers effective — to put qualified, named opportunities in front of them and arm them with collateral that closes work.

What the layer does not do

  • It does not treat inbound lead volume as its primary measure of success.
  • It does not assign one person to each production craft.
  • It does not produce content on a calendar for its own sake, disconnected from what the sellers need.
  • It does not carry a full-time developer for a marketing site that AI-assisted build can maintain.

Keep and grow the clients we win

Winning a client is the start, not the finish. This layer keeps signed clients, grows them, and turns delivered work back into new revenue. What it owns

  • Expansion — a structured land-and-expand motion for additional offshore roles inside existing clients.
  • Referrals — deliberately asking satisfied clients for warm introductions, the strongest channel Brainbox has.
  • Proof — capturing delivered outcomes as case studies the brand and sellers can use.

Taking Brainbox into the US

The US Expansion Project takes Brainbox into the United States market. It runs as a distinct initiative, built with the Corporate Services Director over successive iterations, and produces the United States section of the Sales Playbook and the US adaptation of the Demand Generation Playbook, among other outcomes.

What is settledUS Agents sell into the general-agency market, each reporting to a Master Agent with a defined territory. The US-facing demand-generation motion runs on US hours following a US-adapted Demand Generation Playbook — delivered by the Lead Associate until US demand warrants a dedicated US Associate. The US motion sells to general agencies rather than Australian SMEs, so the ideal customer profile, the channels and the message all differ.

What the US iterations will build

  • The US market and ideal customer profile — who Brainbox sells to, and how it differs from the Australian SME model.
  • The general-agency and master-agent structure — the commercial model for reaching US clients.
  • Regulatory and compliance requirements — licensing, data and contractual obligations.
  • The US demand-generation adaptation — channels, ICP, personas and cadence for the general-agency market.
  • US sales enablement — collateral adapted from the Australian assets.
  • Expansion measures and milestones — from first US appointments through to signed engagements.

Closing · Delivering the transformation

The mechanics of the change

The near-term work is the marketing-capability transformation. It runs in four stages.

  1. Instrument and baseline. Fix attribution in HubSpot before we touch the team. Record the baseline so the transformation can be measured against it.
  2. Redefine the roles. Rewrite the position descriptions for the pod and Associate roles. Confirm reporting lines and the dotted line to the Manager. Document the AI tooling each role will use.
  3. Redeploy and reskill. Move the web developer to client delivery. Build AI capability in the Technologist and Producer roles — their upskilling is the transformation itself.
  4. Operate and prove. Run the new model. Track pipeline contribution against the baseline. Build the marketing engine into a case study. Review at ninety days.

People transitions

The transformation changes roles; it does not default to redundancy. Where skills transfer to billable client work, redeployment keeps capability inside the firm and turns a cost into revenue — and reflects the Brainbox value of always being kind. The web developer redeploys to client delivery. The social media person and graphic designer merge into the single Producer role, settling to one seat as AI fluency builds. Transitions touch two labour regimes — Philippine labour law and the Fair Work framework — and we take advice on both before we act.

Retraining

Retraining will be funded as demand requires — defensible for a transformation driven by capability rather than cost. It carries one risk: demand-driven funding tends to lag the capability build, and two roles cannot afford to lag. The Technologist and the Producer are the transformation.

Ring-fence a small baseline nowThe Technologist needs prompt engineering, AI workflow design and answer-engine optimisation. The Producer needs AI-assisted production fluency across copy, image and video. The rest of the retraining budget stays demand-driven.

Measuring success

The transformation is not measured by headcount. It is measured by capability and contribution across the five layers.

Pipeline contribution

A rising count of qualified, named accounts delivered to the two sellers, tracked against the stage-one baseline.

Enablement readiness

Sellers report that collateral is current and useful at the point of need.

Technology & attribution

The function shows which activity produces pipeline, with evidence from a connected CRM and website.

Brand & relationships

Founder-led thought leadership grows in reach; existing clients expand and refer.

The proof pointBrainbox runs an AI-augmented revenue function from its own delivery centre, feeding sellers across two countries. Few competitors can show a prospect their own offshore engine working. The transformed function is not only cheaper to run or sharper in output — it is evidence. A prospect who questions whether offshoring works for skilled, judgement-heavy work can be shown the firm running exactly that, on itself, every day.
