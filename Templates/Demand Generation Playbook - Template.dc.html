Role purpose & primary objective

The Demand Generation Specialist builds a consistent pipeline of qualified sales appointments for Brainbox. The focus is Western Australian SME companies with 10–100 employees, plus selected Australian and US prospects with a clear fit for offshore services.

Why this role matters The Demand Generation Specialist is the bridge between marketing and sales — turning marketing’s awareness and interest into qualified, sales-ready conversations. The role is considered a key driver of new revenue for Brainbox: the pipeline it creates is where future commercial transactions begin.

The primary output is a booked sales appointment with a suitable decision maker or senior influencer, recorded in HubSpot with a clear business reason, contact history, company profile and recommended sales angle. The target is quality-adjusted, not activity-only — the correct benchmark is appointments held, not appointments booked. Review targets after the first six weeks.

What counts as a qualified appointment

A meeting counts only when all of the following are met:

  • The company fits the Brainbox ideal customer profile.
  • The contact is a decision maker, budget holder, operational owner or credible internal sponsor.
  • The prospect has at least one confirmed or strongly evidenced business driver.
  • The meeting is accepted in the sales team calendar.
  • The HubSpot record contains clear context for the sales person.
  • The prospect was not booked through misleading claims, excessive pressure or vague bait.
Business drivers include: recruitment difficulty, cost pressure, growth, administration overload, a vacant role, delayed work, process failure, poor software utilisation, need for extended hours, need for back-office support, or frustration with local hiring.

Qualify before you book — without over-qualifying

Use only the questions you need. Over-qualifying loses the meeting.

  • Which role or function is creating the most pressure right now?
  • Is this mainly cost, capacity, recruitment difficulty or process backlog?
  • Are you the right person, or someone who manages operations or finance?
  • Have you used offshore or outsourced support before?
  • Would you prefer to discuss admin, finance, customer service, marketing or operations support?

Account selection criteria

Target accounts should meet at least four of the following:

  • 10–100 employees
  • WA-based or with a meaningful WA office
  • Visible admin, finance, marketing, customer service, compliance, estimating, scheduling or ops roles
  • Recently advertised for support staff
  • Multiple office staff visible on LinkedIn
  • Uses offshore-supportable software (Xero, MYOB, HubSpot, Zoho, CargoWise, Simpro, ServiceM8, PropertyMe, Property Tree, Reapit, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Moodle, aXcelerate, SharePoint, Teams)
  • Growth signals: new locations, services, contracts, leadership hires, investment, recruitment
  • Labour-pressure signals: repeated job ads, long vacancies, high-volume admin roles, "urgent hiring" posts
  • Likely recurring process work
  • Owner-led or founder-led
  • Management team small enough to value direct cost & capacity improvement
  • Not too small to afford support, not too large for long enterprise procurement

Pursue

  • Australian SMEs with visible back-office functions
  • Active recruiters for support roles
  • Owner / founder-led businesses
  • Recurring, process-heavy workloads

Exclude

  • Too small / too large for the model
  • Heavily regulated with no offshore-suitable functions
  • Government agencies, charities without budget signals
  • No visible decision makers; poor fit; already in active sales discussions

Sales-executive LinkedIn network growth

Brainbox sales conversations land better when the request comes from the sales executive’s own network. The Demand Generation Specialist is responsible for growing that network on the executive’s behalf.

Mandatory weekly requirement Each week, the Demand Generation Specialist must log in as one of the sales executives (using approved, delegated access) and generate at least 20 new, relevant connections from that executive’s LinkedIn profile — targeting decision makers and influencers that match the Brainbox ICP and the week’s vertical focus.

How to run it

  • Use only delegated access the sales executive has explicitly approved — never undisclosed or shared credentials outside that arrangement.
  • Send personalised connection requests as the executive, matched to the weekly vertical and a relevant trigger.
  • Keep the executive’s voice and tone — warm, human, no pitch in the connection note.
  • Stagger requests through the week to stay within LinkedIn limits and look human.
  • Record each connected contact in HubSpot with source “Sales-exec LinkedIn” and the owning executive.

Standards & guardrails

  • Minimum 20 new accepted-or-pending relevant connection requests per executive profile, per week; quality and fit come before raw volume.
  • No automation tools that breach LinkedIn rules or risk the executive’s account.
  • Respect every opt-out, decline and “do not contact” signal immediately.
  • Hand newly connected contacts into the normal nurture and outreach cadence.
  • Report new connections per executive in the weekly report.
Example connection note (sent as the executive) “Hi [Name], I work with Australian SMEs on building high-performing teams and managed offshore support. I noticed your team is active in [sector] — it would be good to connect.”

Marketing-led campaign tasks

As the bridge between marketing and sales, the Demand Generation Specialist also delivers campaign-specific tasks allocated by the Marketing Manager — event and webinar follow-ups, content and EDM lists, sector campaigns and inbound enquiry qualification.

How campaign work fits the week Campaign tasks are not run separately from the core cadence — they must be cross-referenced into the existing weekly activities and targets. Campaign follow-ups count toward first-touch, follow-up, call and conversation targets; campaign-sourced meetings count toward booked and held appointments.

Each Monday

  • Confirm with the Marketing Manager any live or upcoming campaigns and the specific tasks allocated.
  • Fold campaign accounts and contacts into the week’s target list and tiering — not a parallel queue.
  • Agree priority and any deadlines that differ from the standard cadence.

Through the week

  • Action campaign tasks inside the normal time blocks (research, outreach, calls, conversion).
  • Log campaign touches against both the weekly targets and the campaign tag in HubSpot.
  • Report campaign progress and outcomes back to the Marketing Manager and in the weekly report.

Tag every campaign contact

  • Original channel — event, content, EDM, paid or referral.
  • Lifecycle stage and lead status, kept current.

Day-by-day cadence

Monday — plan & build

Monday creates control of the week: define who will be targeted, why they are likely to care, and what appointment outcome is expected.

BlockAction
8:30–9:00Sales alignment. Review last week’s booked & held meetings, capture sales feedback on lead quality, agree the weekly sector focus, message theme, appointment capacity and the case studies / offers to use.
9:00–10:30Target account build. Build / refresh 20–30 priority accounts via Sales Navigator, Apollo, HubSpot, job ads, directories and referral lists. Filter on WA, 10–100 staff, the weekly vertical, private ownership, stable/positive headcount, recent hiring and visible support functions.
10:30–11:30Account scoring (see model below). Tier 1 (80+) gets multi-threaded outreach this week; Tier 2 (60–79) enters nurture with selected direct outreach; Tier 3 (40–59) is held for a trigger.
11:30–12:30Buyer mapping. Identify 3–5 contacts per Tier 1 account — at least one senior decision maker and one operational influencer. Record role, LinkedIn, email, phone, source, confidence, buying influence and proposed angle.
1:00–2:30Trigger research. Find the specific reason to contact each priority account (hiring, growth, capacity, cost, process, leadership, service or content trigger). Record the exact trigger — never generic assumptions.
2:30–3:30Message prep. Create a personalised angle per Tier 1 account and three weekly message variations (Owner/MD, Operations/GM, Finance/HR). Connect to a business issue, not a generic outsourcing pitch.
3:30–4:30HubSpot set-up. Create tasks & sequences, account notes, lifecycle stage, lead status, next-activity date, vertical & pain tags, owner; check for duplicates and prior “do not contact” status.
4:30–5:00Close-out. Send the sales lead a summary: vertical focus, Tier 1 count, contacts mapped, best five accounts to watch, main triggers, planned Tuesday first-touch volume, and any data / tool issues.
Hints & tips. Build the list before writing a single message — targeting beats volume. Bank three reusable angles per vertical so Tuesday starts fast. If an account has no clear trigger, it is not a Monday priority.

2:00–3:00LinkedIn engagement. Comment thoughtfully, react, follow pages, save leads/accounts, find referral paths. No mass-liking, no automated comments, no public pitching.
3:00–4:00Appointment conversion. Reply to all; book into the sales calendar with a clear title, agenda, video link, HubSpot association and handover note.
4:00–5:00Clean-up & prep. Update statuses, mark bounces/unsubscribes, create Wednesday tasks, prepare the next 20–30 contacts.

Would it be worth a short call to compare the role with an offshore support model?”
Hints & tips. Clear replies before new outreach — a warm reply outranks any cold send. Keep first touches under five sentences and lead with the trigger, not the service. Call the people who just accepted or opened first.

2:30–3:30Trigger expansion. Find new triggers in the same vertical via job boards, news, posts, tenders and association updates; add strong-signal contacts.

Thursday — conversion day

Turn interest into appointments and create urgency before the week ends.

BlockAction

1:00–2:00Referral mining. Find warm paths into target accounts; prepare specific, easy-to-action referral requests for directors, sales team or partners.
2:00–3:30Recycled lead campaign. Build a reactivation list of 10–20 contacts (closed-lost 6+ months, old enquiries, “not now”, role-changers, recent hirers, dormant right-industry) and send short, specific reactivation outreach.
3:30–4:30Objection handling & nurture. Work replies, set nurture dates, and record objections needing sales input.
4:30–5:00Close-out. Report appointments booked, warm leads not yet booked, referral requests required and Friday priorities.
Booking language “Would Tuesday at 10:00am or Wednesday at 2:00pm suit for a 20-minute call with [sales person]?” · “Would it help to include the person who manages recruitment or operations?”
Hints & tips. Offer two specific times, never “when suits you”. Send the invite while still on the call. Reactivation is the cheapest pipeline you have — work it every week.

Friday — protect quality & prepare

Friday protects quality — convert late-week opportunities, clean data, review performance and prepare next week.

BlockAction
8:30–9:00Meeting confirmation. Confirm next week’s meetings, reminders, invites, handover notes and decision-maker attendance.
9:00–10:30Final follow-up. Polite close-the-loop messages — no pressure.

12:00–12:30Compliance check. Confirm no unsubscribed contacts were messaged, sender details & unsubscribe present, sources recorded, no unauthorised automation or uncontrolled CSV exports.
1:00–3:00Performance review & sales feedback. Review the week’s metrics, then meet the sales lead on meeting quality, fit and targeting.
3:00–5:00Next-week prep & report. Prepare the first 10–15 accounts and saved searches; send the weekly report to management.
Hints & tips. Confirm next week’s meetings before anything else — a held meeting beats a booked one. Leave no active lead without a next step. End the week with next week already built.

Objection handling

How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting, not winning the point.
ObjectionResponse angle
“We are too small.”That is often why this works — one offshore role removes repeatable work without building a larger local management structure.
“We tried outsourcing before.”Useful context. Brainbox is a managed offshore staffing model, not a task marketplace — the difference is recruitment, supervision, process design and retention.
“We only hire locally.”That makes sense for client-facing or site-based work. Brainbox usually supports the process work behind local staff, not the local relationship roles.
“We do not have budget.”The starting point is comparing current local hiring cost, overtime, delays and management load against an offshore support role.
“Send me information.”Happy to send a short summary — it may be more useful to first understand whether you’re looking at admin, finance, customer service, marketing or operations support.
“Not now.”Understood. Is this worth revisiting in three months, or should I close the loop?
“We’re happy as we are.”Great to hear — most of our clients were too. They added support to protect that, not to fix a problem. Worth a 15-minute compare?
“What about data security?”Fair question. Brainbox staff work under managed access, supervision and clear policies — happy to walk your team through exactly how that works.
“We’ll sort it internally.”Understandable. It usually comes down to timing and management load — offshore support often bridges the gap until that internal hire lands.

Your toolkit & how it fits together

Five tools carry the role. Each has a clear job — the value is in the handoff between them: discover, enrich, draft, record, send, then record again.

LinkedIn & Sales Navigator

Discover and research accounts and decision makers, watch for triggers and warm paths, engage socially, and grow the sales executives’ networks (20+ new connections each week).

Apollo

Find, filter, enrich and validate contacts — verified emails, direct dials and current titles — then push only action-ready contacts to HubSpot. Review bounces daily; never dump large lists.

Claude

Synthesise account research, draft personalised first-touch and follow-up variations, summarise prospects and build clean handover notes — faster output that keeps the warm, kind Brainbox tone. Always review before sending.

HubSpot

The source of truth. Every account, contact, task, sequence, lifecycle stage and outcome lives here, with the reporting and dashboards that track pipeline through to revenue.

Outlook

Compliant email outreach and the calendar — send sender-identified email, book discovery meetings directly into the sales team’s calendars, and issue invites with the agenda and video link.

The interplay — one flow

1 · LinkedIn / Sales Navigator — discover & trigger 2 · Apollo — enrich & validate 3 · Claude — research & draft 4 · HubSpot — record, task & sequence 5 · Outlook — send & book 6 · HubSpot — log, report & hand over

HubSpot sits at the centre: Apollo and LinkedIn feed clean records in, Claude shapes the message, Outlook executes the touch and the booking, and every result returns to HubSpot — so nothing is lost between marketing, the specialist and sales.

Golden rules

  • One system of record. If it isn’t in HubSpot, it didn’t happen.
  • Enrich before you send. Apollo confidence checked; no guessed personal emails.
  • Claude drafts, you decide. Always review and personalise before sending.
  • LinkedIn stays human. No automation that risks an account or the brand.
  • Outlook sends, HubSpot logs. Every email and meeting tracked or logged.
  • Close the loop daily. Bounces, opt-outs and replies cleared before new work.

What each stage is for

Foundation · Wk 1–2

Learn the market, tools, systems, messaging and qualification standard. Protect data accuracy above raw volume.

Ramp · Wk 3–4

Increase activity while protecting quality. Hold task completion at 90% or higher.

Performance · Wk 5–6

Establish predictable appointment production with a meeting show rate above 70%.

Steady state · Wk 7+

Optimise conversion and reduce wasted activity; sustain a show rate above 75%.

Quality & compliance standards

Every message is

  • Relevant, short, specific, respectful
  • Easy to answer and accurate
  • Linked to a clear business reason
  • Consistent with “Always Be Kind”
  • Something that would not embarrass Brainbox if forwarded

Every message avoids

  • Generic outsourcing spam & long service lists
  • Unverified claims, fake urgency, false familiarity
  • Guilt-based language
  • Overuse of automation & aggressive follow-up
  • Any repeated contact after opt-out

Data & compliance controls

  • Record source for every contact; record opt-outs immediately.
  • Never contact unsubscribed contacts.
  • Do not collect unnecessary or sensitive personal information.
  • Do not use personal social media or scraped data from unapproved sources.
  • No uncontrolled CSV exports outside approved systems.
  • Use approved sending tools, sender ID and unsubscribe mechanisms.
  • Review bounces daily; suppress invalid emails; escalate complaints.
  • Follow platform rules for LinkedIn, Apollo and HubSpot.

Measures of success

Final success measure The number of leads that turn into sales opportunities and subsequently become a commercial transaction. Every other metric below is a leading indicator of this single revenue outcome — the reason the role exists as a key driver of new revenue for Brainbox.

Primary

  • Qualified appointments booked
  • Qualified appointments held
  • Appointments → opportunities
  • Opportunities → proposals
  • Proposals → clients

Secondary

  • Positive reply & call connection rate
  • LinkedIn acceptance & referral rate
  • No-show & disqualification rate
  • Time from first touch to appointment
  • Activity completion & data accuracy

Quality

  • Sales acceptance of leads
  • Meeting relevance & correct persona
  • Clear pain point & complete handovers
  • Low complaint / unsubscribe / bounce rate
  • Few duplicate records

Review cadence: a short daily end-of-day note, a 30-minute weekly review with the sales lead, and a 60-minute monthly performance & market review with management.

Reporting cadence

Daily note

First touches, follow-ups, calls, conversations, appointments, warm replies, issues needing help and tomorrow’s priority.

Weekly review · 30 min

Appointments booked & held, meeting quality, conversion by sector, message performance, objections, data quality, next-week priorities and sales support needed.

Monthly review · 60 min

Appointment trends, revenue conversion, sector & source performance, sales feedback, data & compliance, tool performance, campaign & messaging changes and market refinements.
