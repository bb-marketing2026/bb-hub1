1.1 · Purpose of this manual

The shared picture — what we look after, the systems that govern every decision, and the daily, weekly and monthly rhythms that keep it all predictable.

This manual exists to provide certainty. It removes ambiguity from daily operations, defines how work flows through the Fleet & Facilities Department, and establishes who does what, when, and why.

Facilities work often fails when it relies on memory, goodwill or constant verbal instruction. This manual replaces those habits with structure. It creates a rhythm that is predictable, auditable and repeatable — so good outcomes don’t depend on any one person remembering the right thing at the right moment.

Who it applies to

All Fleet & Facilities staff — during normal operations, peak demand and weekends, and regardless of individual availability, workload pressure or competing priorities.

When you may deviate

Only where safety, legal compliance or guest welfare requires it. Every deviation is recorded and reviewed — never made quietly.

1.2 · Assets & functions we manage

The department operates across four environments. Each has a different demand profile and tolerance for delay — but all are managed through the same functions and the same systems.

BBT & BBN commercial buildings

Two buildings, both operating 24×7. Shared spaces are heavily used at lunch and shift changes; small lapses are noticed quickly. Work here is preventative. Only BBT has parking, and the vehicle fleet is stored there.

Residential & short-stay

Ten residential properties, five run as short-stay. Guest expectations are high and tolerance for defects low. Work is time-bound: check-in readiness is not flexible and cleaning windows are fixed.

Vehicle fleet

Vehicles support staff transport and operations. Stored and managed at BBT, with Facilities responsible for protection, presentation, availability and safety. Repairs are escalated, not improvised.

One standard, everywhere

However different the environments feel, they share one safety standard, one set of systems, and one chain of accountability through the Facilities Manager.

The functions we deliverBuilding: maintenance, utilities, safety, access, cleaning, consumables, asset renewal. Fleet: driver & route management, rostering, maintenance, safety, cleaning, repairs, asset renewal. Daily work removes risk, weekly work prevents backlog, monthly work keeps assets safe and compliant, triggered work responds to bookings and faults.

1.3 · Systems of record

Two systems govern every decision. Discipline in how we use them is what makes the whole operation visible and auditable. If a system is wrong, it is corrected before work proceeds — personal judgement does not override it.

Uplisting

The authoritative source for accommodation activity — arrival dates and times, length of stay, same-day turnovers, and third-day cleaning triggers for long stays. Cleaning schedules are derived from Uplisting data alone.

Asana

The single work register. Maintenance issues, cleaning observations, guest & internal requests and security concerns are all logged here. Work is not actioned unless it is logged; verbal requests become tasks before work begins.

The work-control workflow

Step 1 · Intake
Raised & routed to Jho

All requests funnel to Jho — the single point of contact.

Step 2 · Log
Logged in Asana

Clear, categorised task with description and photos; no duplicates.

Step 3 · Triage
Prioritised by May

May sets priority and direction; Jho coordinates under it.

Step 4 · Action
Actioned or escalated

Resolved within authority, or stabilised and escalated to a contractor.

Step 5 · Close
Closed & reviewed

Closed only on confirmation; patterns reviewed so faults are designed out.

The ruleIf it isn’t in Asana, it isn’t happening. A task gives every piece of work an owner, a history and a finish line — that’s what makes the department accountable rather than busy.

1.4 · Operating philosophy & escalation

A few principles sit underneath every decision. They explain why the rhythms in the rest of this manual are shaped the way they are.

Readiness first

Short-stay readiness takes priority over all other work. Fixed time windows are protected. The daily plan is locked once — replanning is the exception.

Use the hours well

Early hours reduce risk before buildings get busy; later hours manage outcomes. Noise is avoided; visibility maintained throughout.

Log once, track to close

Problems are logged once, tracked until closed, and reviewed for patterns. Reporting exists to drive change — never produced for its own sake.

How a problem moves

Observed (reported to Jho, not fixed quietly) → Logged in Asana (with description & photos) → Triaged by May against guest readiness, safety and business continuity, who chooses:

Resolve

Fixed within authority & skill by Arvin or the team.

Stabilise

Made safe now; contractor engaged for full repair.

Escalate

Contractor or expenditure approved by May.

Closed on confirmationThe task is closed only when completion is confirmed — never assumed. Critical safety items are confirmed personally, and recurring faults are surfaced in weekly and monthly reviews so the root cause is addressed.

1.6 · Accommodation cleaning framework

Accommodation cleaning follows strict rules that prevent scope creep and overload. With two cleaners, properties can be turned around in parallel — but only the properties the rules authorise, and only within the fixed window.

Trigger
Uplisting flags it

A same-day turnover, or a long-stay guest (over 3 days) reaching the next third-day clean.

Confirm
Jho extracts the list

The day’s authorised properties are confirmed from Uplisting and put to May for lock-in.

Clean
Venus & Ivy, 11:00–1:40

Depart BBT on time; clean only authorised properties, in parallel.

Return
Back by 1:40pm

Cleaning ceases in time to return for building cleaning; the window is never extended.

Report
Observations to Jho

Defects seen during cleaning are reported and logged — reported, not fixed.

The fixed window

All accommodation cleaning happens strictly between 11:00am and 1:40pm. Travel time is planned and protected. The window is not compressed or extended — attempts to do so create downstream failure.

Laundry & linen

Handled by property, washed, dried, folded and stored to a consistent standard. Damaged or stained items are removed from circulation, and shortages reported promptly. Quality is maintained; improvisation avoided.

Cleaning triggersOn turnover (same-day check-out and check-in) · long-stay guests over 3 days (cleaned every third day). No other cleaning is permitted unless guest-paid and approved by May — this keeps capacity aligned with demand.

1.7 · Daily operating rhythm

The day runs to a known shape: remove risk early, lock the plan once, protect the fixed windows, and close cleanly. The concierge desk stays covered throughout.

6:00am
Early operations & vehicle readiness

Jong uncovers and safety-checks the fleet at BBT; Arvin runs early inspections across BBT and BBN. Focus is safety, access and urgent defects; anything unresolved is escalated to May.

6:30–8:00
Morning staff transport

Jong runs the approved routes on time. Arvin covers early access and opens the concierge desk so the forecourt is managed as vehicles arrive.

8:30am
Pre-start control

On-site presence at the BBT concierge desk. Jho reviews Uplisting — arrivals, stay lengths, cleaning triggers — and prepares a concise summary for May.

9:00am
Manager triage & lock-in

May works through the summary with Jho. Decisions made once: which properties clean, check-in attendance, cleaner timing, May’s 2–6 availability, priority maintenance. Asana updated; plan locked.

9:20am
Stand-up

A short stand-up for confirmation, not debate. Each person confirms understanding; no new work is introduced.

11:00–1:40
Accommodation cleaning

Venus and Ivy clean only authorised properties, in parallel, returning by 1:40. Jho monitors guest messages; concierge desk stays covered.

2:00–3:00
Building cleaning — BBT & BBN

The team restores Levels 2–5 across both buildings, clearing lunch-period residue in two 24×7 environments.

2:00–6:00
Check-in control window

May attends residential check-ins/outs and guest liaison for visiting clients. Jho manages messaging; Arvin and Jong hold the forecourt. Internal meetings avoided.

End of day
Clean close

Jho prepares the daily report; May reviews exceptions only. Open issues roll forward visibly in Asana.

1.12 · Asset register & renewal

Every major asset is recorded, reviewed on a fixed cycle, and carried on a renewal plan — so replacement is budgeted and deliberate, never a surprise. The register is the single source of truth for what we own, its condition, and when it is due for renewal.

Step 1
Verify

Walk the register against the assets — confirm each exists, its location and condition.

Step 2
Record movements

Log assets purchased, replaced or written off since the last review.

Step 3
Re-rate & forecast

Update expected life and condition; reset renewal year and estimate.

Step 4
Plan & budget

Refresh the renewal plan; feed forecast spend into the budget cycle.

PolicyNo major asset is purchased, replaced or written off outside the approved budget and policy. Write-offs are documented with reason and authorisation; every active major asset carries a current renewal year. May and Jho review the full register twice a year.

1.13–1.14 · Non-negotiable rules & outcome

  1. Uplisting governs accommodation cleaning — the only booking source.
  2. Asana governs all work control — the only work register. If it isn’t logged, it isn’t actioned.
  3. All issues, complaints and requests route through Jho.

  7. Vehicle licensing and maintenance never lapse; unsafe vehicles are not operated.
  8. Weekend work is exception-based; the monthly safety inspection is mandatory.
Operating outcomeThis manual exists to create calm. When it is followed, the department becomes predictable. Predictability builds trust, and trust reduces escalation. Structure is what lets us be kind under pressure — to guests, to colleagues, and to ourselves.

3.2 · Monthly safety & compliance inspection

For a multi-level commercial building in Angeles City, Philippines. Inspect every level. Log all findings in Asana, prioritise corrective actions, and confirm critical items closed.

1 Fire safety & emergency

  • Extinguishers present, charged, tagged & in date
  • Fire alarm panel normal; detectors functional
  • Emergency & exit lighting illuminate on test
  • Exits, stairwells & corridors clear and unlocked
  • Fire doors self-latch; evacuation plan posted
  • Valid FSIC on file

2 Electrical & power

  • Panels closed, labelled, accessible
  • No exposed wiring, scorching or overheating
  • Breakers/RCD tested; no persistent tripping
  • Outlets secure; no overloaded boards
  • Generator starts & carries load; ATS operates

3 Vertical transport & access

  • Lift inspection certificate current
  • Lift emergency phone/alarm works
  • Stairwell handrails secure; treads non-slip
  • Common areas & car park well lit
  • Access control / CCTV operational

4 Water, sanitation & HVAC

  • Water pumps & pressure normal; tanks clean
  • No leaks, damp or staining (check after rain)
  • Drainage clear; no pooling
  • Comfort rooms clean, stocked, functional
  • Aircon serviced; filters clean

5 Structural & general

  • Walls, ceilings, floors free of new cracks
  • Waterproofing & roof sound
  • Glazing, balustrades & railings secure
  • Signage, fixtures & finishes intact
  • Car park markings, barriers & bollards in order

6 Health, pest & documents

  • Pest control current; no infestation
  • Garbage area clean, segregated & collected
  • First-aid kits stocked & in date
  • Occupancy, business & sanitary permits current
  • Previous inspection actions closed out
Critical itemsAny fire-safety, electrical or structural failure is logged immediately, prioritised, and confirmed closed before the inspection is signed off. Inspection conducted by May, Betina and Arvin.

3.3 · Fleet vehicle — weekly maintenance check

Completed by the driver each week with the engine cold. Walk around the vehicle first, then check from the cab. Report any defect to Jho for logging in Asana — do not operate an unsafe or unroadworthy vehicle.

1 Exterior & body

  • Bodywork free of new dents/damage
  • Head, tail, brake & reverse lights work
  • Indicators & hazards work front and rear
  • Plates secure, clean & legible
  • Mirrors intact; windscreen free of cracks; wipers work

2 Tyres & wheels

  • Tread depth legal; even wear
  • Pressures correct (incl. spare), checked cold
  • No cuts, bulges or embedded objects
  • Wheel nuts secure; no missing nuts/caps

3 Under bonnet

  • Engine oil correct level & clean
  • Coolant between min/max
  • Brake & clutch fluid at level
  • Washer fluid topped up
  • Battery secure; belts & hoses intact; no leaks

4 Cab & controls

  • All seatbelts latch & retract
  • Horn works; warning lights clear
  • Air-con & demister work
  • Interior clean; no loose items
  • Registration & insurance in vehicle; fuel adequate

5 Operation & brakes

  • Foot brake firm; pulls up straight
  • Handbrake holds on an incline
  • Steering responsive; no pull or vibration
  • Gear selection / transmission smooth

4.2 · Asana conventions

A consistent queue is a readable queue. These conventions keep tasks findable, sortable and free of duplicates — so the work register stays the single source of truth.

Task naming

Every task follows the same shape: [Site] · [Category] — short description. E.g. “BBT · Electrical — L3 kitchen power point sparking”; “Unit 5 · Plumbing — shower draining slowly”; “Fleet · Vehicle — HiAce front tyre wear”.

Categories

Maintenance · Electrical · Plumbing · Cleaning · Consumables · Safety · Access & security · Traffic / concierge · Vehicle · Guest request · Asset renewal · Admin.

Every task carries

  • Name in the standard format
  • Category & priority (P1–P4) set
  • Assignee and due date

4.3 · Vehicle accident & incident

If a fleet vehicle is involved in an accident or incident, follow these steps in order. Safety first, then secure the scene, then record everything. Do not admit fault or agree to private settlement.

  1. Make safe & check for injuries. Stop immediately. Check for injury; if anyone is hurt, call emergency services first. Hazard lights on; set out the warning triangle.
  2. Call for help. Where there is injury, danger or a crime, call emergency services. Then notify May immediately — do not wait until you return.
  3. Do not admit fault. Stay calm and polite. Do not accept blame, sign anything, or agree to a private cash settlement. Exchange details only.
  4. Exchange & gather details. Record the other driver’s name, contact, licence, plate, vehicle and insurer; note witnesses and the attending officer.
  5. Photograph everything. All vehicles, damage, plates, the wider scene, road conditions and any injuries. More is better.
  6. Report & log. Complete the incident report form, give it to May, and log a P1 task in Asana so insurance and repair follow-up is tracked to closure.
Incident report form capturesDriver · vehicle/rego · date & time · location · weather/road conditions · injuries · police attended & officer ref · other party name/contact/plate/insurer · sequence of events · photos taken · Asana task no. · time reported to May · signatures.
