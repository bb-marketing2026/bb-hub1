Purpose

This Sales Playbook is the operational manual for the sales-execution layer of the Revenue Playbook. It documents how the Commercial Manager converts a qualified opportunity into a signed engagement in the Australian market. The US motion is scaffolded in the Revenue Playbook and documented separately as the US Expansion Project matures.

It sits beneath the Revenue Playbook and alongside its siblings — the Marketing Playbook, the Social Media Playbook and the Demand Generation Playbook. Where those documents own brand, demand and the booked appointment, this one owns everything from the qualified appointment onward: discovery, scoping, proposal, negotiation and close.

The networking standard

Non-negotiableThe Commercial Manager attends a minimum of five networking meetings every week across the organisations above. Networking is the firm’s largest channel and is treated as core delivery, not optional.

How networking is worked

  • Plan the week — select the five-plus events at the weekly orchestration meeting, matched to where ideal-profile owners gather.
  • Show up to give — lead with usefulness and the Brainbox point of view, not a pitch (Always Be Kind).
  • Capture — every worthwhile contact logged in HubSpot the same day, tagged source “Networking” and the organisation.
  • Convert — follow up within 24 hours; move genuine fits to a discovery meeting.
  • Report — meetings attended and opportunities surfaced reviewed weekly with the CSD.
Why fiveConsistent presence compounds — five meetings a week keeps Brainbox visible across the networks that produce most of its work, and gives the CSD a clear, countable activity baseline.

Section 3 · Engagement methodology

Consultative selling with SPIN

Brainbox sells consultatively. The seller uses SPIN to run discovery — drawing out the client’s situation and problems, making the implications real, and letting the client articulate the value of solving them. We diagnose before we prescribe.

S · Situation

Understand the business: team size and roles, the back-office and admin load, systems in use, and how they hire and resource today.

P · Problem

Surface the difficulties: recruitment difficulty, cost pressure, capacity limits, turnover, overtime, delayed work, process gaps.

I · Implication

Make the cost real: senior people on low-value admin, growth held back, service slipping, margin eroded, burnout.

N · Need-payoff

Let the client state the value: freed capacity, faster turnaround, cost versus a local hire, the relief of reliable support.

The ruleTalk less, ask better. The best discovery has the client describing their own problem and its cost — the seller confirms fit and authority, and only then frames the offshore solution.

The discovery question bank

Examples the seller adapts — not a script. Use only what the conversation needs.

StageExample questions
SituationHow is your team structured today? Which roles carry the admin and back-office work? What systems do you run (Xero, HubSpot, CRM, job management)? How do you currently resource peaks?
ProblemWhere does capacity run out first? How hard is it to hire and keep good admin or support staff locally? What work keeps slipping? Where are you paying overtime or leaving work undone?
ImplicationWhat does it cost when that work is late? How much senior time goes on tasks a support person could do? What growth are you turning away? What does the turnover cost you to replace?
Need-payoffIf a trained person handled that reliably, what would it free your team to do? What would faster turnaround be worth? How would it help to compare that against a local hire?
Confirm before you proposeBefore scoping, the seller confirms the four qualifiers: fit to the ideal profile, a real need, budget authority, and timing. A proposal without these is effort spent on a deal that will stall.

Section 6 · The offer

The offer & how it is priced

Brainbox places dedicated, managed offshore personnel from its Philippines delivery centre. The offer is simple to price and easy to compare.

Unit
Priced per dedicated offshore role, at the published per-role rate.
Value anchor
Compared against the client’s loaded local cost for the equivalent role — salary, on-costs, super, recruitment, management and overhead.
Typical value
Around $42,000 per annum per role; most clients start with one to two roles.
Term
12-month minimum term, reflecting the managed recruitment, training and supervision behind each placement.

How the seller frames it

  • Work the relevant role capability map with the buyer to scope the need accurately and agree a starting trust level (see next page).
  • Lead with the client’s own need-payoff language from discovery, not a feature list.
  • Build a side-by-side cost comparison — offshore role versus loaded local cost — in the proposal.
  • Reinforce that this is a managed model: recruitment, training, supervision, retention and cover, not a freelance marketplace.
  • Use the firm itself as proof — Brainbox runs its own marketing and demand engine offshore.
Delegated authorityStandard per-role pricing and the 12-month term sit within the seller’s authority. Discounts, non-standard terms or unusual scope go to the CSD.

Role capability maps

Capability map · five trust levels

Every role scoping activity uses the relevant role capability map. It is how the seller guides a buyer to an accurate representation of their need — neither under-scoped nor over-promised.

A capability map takes a role — Bookkeeper, Executive Assistant, HR Support Officer, and others as they are built — and lays out everything it can do across a five-level trust model. Each function is read left to right, from routine, reviewed work through to fully delegated, fiduciary responsibility. The buyer chooses the level to start at, and raises it as confidence builds.

The five levels of trust

Trust grows →
1
Reliable hands
Routine, reviewed work
2
Standing access
Recurring tasks, lightly checked
3
Gatekeeper
Owns the ledger or process
4
Delegated authority
Acts within agreed limits
5
Full trust
Fiduciary, in your name

Why it guides accurate scoping

  • Buyers routinely under-describe the need (“just data entry”) or over-assume (“they’d run our finances”). The map shows the real breadth and depth of the role.
  • Walking the buyer across each function surfaces work they had not thought to delegate — accurate scope, not a guess.
  • Choosing a starting level right-sizes the engagement and the per-role price, and sets a clear growth path as trust builds — the natural handover to Relationship management for expansion.
  • It anchors the conversation in capability and trust, not cost — reinforcing the managed, consultative model.
Required in every scopeThe seller works the relevant capability map with the buyer during discovery and scoping, agrees the starting trust level for each function, and records it in the proposal. Maps are maintained in the Sales Enablement Catalog and grow as new roles are added.

Sales adopts AI as a staged 30/60/90-day journey, built around a single Claude proposal-generation skill. The relationship, the pricing judgement and the final send always stay human.

The AI journey — 60 & 90 days

The skill moves from live and baselined to fully embedded in the proposal chain, measured against the day-30 baseline.

60 daysHubSpot · Level alignment

All proposals in the past 30 days used the skill. HubSpot deal data pre-fills context and turnaround is logged per deal. The skill also calibrates the position description to the level discussed, so what the client pays for matches what the role looks like.

  • Review skill-produced proposals: what’s consistently edited? Refine the prompt.
  • HubSpot deal data connected to reduce manual briefing input.
  • Objection-handling and follow-up templates added to the Project.
  • Level-alignment check: the skill tunes the position-description language and years-of-experience to the commercial-model tier agreed with the client, so price, seniority and expectations match before it goes out.
MeasureFaster average proposal turnaround vs current. % of proposals using the skill (target: 100%). Position descriptions aligned to the agreed commercial level.
90 daysProposal chain · Output

The full chain runs: HubSpot deal data and discovery notes in, Claude builds and level-aligns the proposal, then outputs to the chosen destination — the tokenised PandaDoc template, or a repeatable Claude Design proposal if PandaDoc was retired. A person does QA and sends, every time.

  • Output skill live: Claude populates the tokenised PandaDoc template, or builds the proposal in Claude Design if PandaDoc was retired.
  • Post-discovery-call skill feeds the chain: notes in, proposal brief out, into Claude’s draft.
  • Human step kept: QA and send stay with a person — no auto-send.
  • Report volume, turnaround and win rate for AI-assisted vs the prior period; Sales presents at the showcase.
MeasureFaster turnaround. Proposal volume. Win rate. Hours saved per deal. % built through the HubSpot → Claude → proposal-output chain.

What good looks like

The sales function succeeds when the numbers are met and the standard is held.

The numbers

2–3 signed engagements per seller per month at a 25% win rate; pipeline coverage of 3× the target; forecast accuracy improving quarter on quarter.

The standard

Five-plus networking meetings a week; HubSpot current; handovers honoured; proposals within five days; no deal without a next step.

The final measurePer the Revenue Playbook, success is leads that become signed, commercial engagements. Every stage, cadence and channel in this playbook is a leading indicator of that one outcome.
