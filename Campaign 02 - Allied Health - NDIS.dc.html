Why this industry

The pressure

Clinics must maintain service quality while managing intake, scheduling, billing, report administration, referral follow-up and client communication. Most don't need another clinician to fix that — they need stronger administrative capacity around the clinicians they already have.

The opportunity

The best offshore use cases are structured and process-driven: intake, scheduling, NDIS plan admin, billing support, referral tracking, document formatting and client follow-up — support around clinicians without touching clinical judgement, treatment decisions or regulated responsibilities.

The labour market they live in

Allied health and disability services are among WA's fastest-growing sectors, but demand has outrun the supply of both clinicians and experienced practice administrators. Clinics compete hard for reception, intake and billing staff, and turnover in those roles is high — every departure disrupts the schedule and the cash flow.

Why the admin load hurts

Clinical revenue depends on a chain of administration: intake and consent, scheduling and reminders, Medicare and private billing, NDIS plan management, service agreements, referral follow-up and report formatting. When admin slips, cancellations rise, claims stall, debtors grow and clinicians end up doing paperwork instead of billable client work.

The pressures shaping their year

NDIS pricing and compliance change frequently, margins are thin, and no-shows and unbilled sessions hit small clinics hard. Owner-clinicians often carry the practice-management load themselves, and burnout is real. They want to see more clients without adding another expensive local hire.

Where offshore support fits

Clinical judgement, treatment and regulated decisions stay with the local team. A dedicated offshore administrator handles the structured, process-driven work — intake, reminders, billing, referral tracking, document formatting and debtor follow-up — improving clinic flow and freeing clinicians for client care.

Talk like an insider

Know the language: intake, triage, plan management, service agreements, self-managed vs plan-managed vs agency-managed, NDIS line items, Medicare EPC, DVA, no-shows and DNAs. Know the tools: Cliniko, Halaxy, Coreplus, Splose, PowerDiary. Respect privacy and clinical boundaries in every sentence.

Objection handling — allied health & ndis

How to handle any objection. Acknowledge it sincerely, reframe to the business issue, then ask one question that keeps the conversation open. Never argue and never oversell — the goal is a short meeting for the Commercial Manager, not winning the point on the call.
ObjectionResponse angle
“This is clinical — I can't offshore it.”Completely agree — clinical work stays with your team. We only support the administration around it: intake, reminders, billing, referral follow-up. Which of those is heaviest right now?
“Client data privacy worries me.”A fair concern in health. Brainbox staff work under managed access, confidentiality agreements and clear policies, inside your systems — happy to walk your team through exactly how.
“NDIS billing is too complex.”It is — which is why a trained, dedicated person who learns your line items and processes beats a rotating local temp. The rules stay owned by you; the processing gets consistent.
“We tried outsourcing and it flopped.”Usually a scoping and supervision issue. Our managed model recruits, trains and supervises the role and covers leave — very different from a task marketplace.
“We're a small clinic.”Most of our health clients are small — one part-time offshore administrator removes the after-hours admin an owner-clinician is carrying, without another local hire.
“Just email me something.”Happy to — is intake, billing or referral follow-up your bigger pressure? I'll send the most relevant example.
Know when to stop. A clear "no" frees time for better-fit accounts. Close the loop kindly, set a nurture date if there's a future trigger, and respect every opt-out immediately.
