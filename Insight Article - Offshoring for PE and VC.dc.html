Why a fund should require, sponsor or recommend offshoring across its portfolio — and how the maths turns operating savings into exit value.

The argument in short

Private equity and venture capital no longer make their returns on financial engineering. They make them on operational improvement inside the hold period. Offshoring is one of the few levers that is fast to deploy, works in almost any portfolio company, and compounds at exit.

Sustainable cost removed from a portfolio company is EBITDA. EBITDA is what the company is valued on. So a saving made in year one is paid for again, at a multiple, on the day you sell.

Three reasons this belongs on the value-creation agenda

Fast
A defined role can be scoped, recruited and productive within weeks — not the quarters a restructure takes.
Portable
The same playbook works across services, healthcare, logistics, technology and professional firms.
Compounding
Prove it in one company, then repeat it in the next. The second deployment is far cheaper than the first.

The economics

A saving made once is paid for twice

How operating savings become exit value

Every investor already knows this arithmetic. It is worth stating plainly because it is the entire reason offshoring deserves a place on the value-creation agenda rather than the cost-cutting one.

The mechanism

1

Cost is removed from the operating base

A defined set of repeatable tasks — finance administration, reporting, scheduling, customer support, data work — is performed by dedicated offshore staff at a materially lower fully-loaded cost.

2

The saving is sustainable, not one-off

Because the work still gets done, and gets done to a defined standard, the reduction survives diligence. It appears as a structural change in the operating base, not a deferred expense.

3

EBITDA rises by the amount saved

Nothing else needs to change. No new revenue, no price increase, no market risk. The saving flows straight to the line the company is valued on.

4

The exit multiple does the rest

At sale the buyer applies a multiple to EBITDA. Whatever multiple your sector commands, the year-one saving is capitalised into the sale price.

The second-order effectCapacity released is as valuable as cost removed. When a general manager stops doing administration, they return to customers, pricing and delivery — the activities that grow revenue. Most portfolio companies feel this benefit before they feel the cost one.
Where it does not apply. If a company’s back office is already lean, offshore, or genuinely inseparable from client delivery, the lever is weak — and we will say so. The model works best where a growing business has accumulated administrative load faster than it has structured it.

Where offshoring fits in the playbook

Most funds already run a value-creation playbook: pricing, procurement, sales effectiveness, systems, working capital. Offshoring sits alongside these as a labour-structure lever, and it is usually the least contested of the set because nobody loses their job for it when it is scoped to growth.

Functions that transfer well

FunctionWhat moves offshore
Finance & accountingAccounts payable and receivable, reconciliations, payroll processing, month-end preparation, management reporting.
AdministrationData entry and hygiene, document control, scheduling, order processing, compliance paperwork.
Customer operationsInbound and outbound support, ticket triage, onboarding administration, retention follow-up.
Sales supportCRM discipline, quote preparation, proposal formatting, pipeline reporting, lead research.
Technical & specialistEngineering drafting support, estimating and take-offs, clinical and allied-health administration, assessment and marking.
Fund-level supportDeal screening, market mapping, comparable analysis, data-book preparation, portfolio KPI packs, LP reporting.

Strong candidates

  • Repeatable, documented or documentable work
  • High volume, moderate complexity
  • Measurable output and clear quality standards
  • Work currently done by expensive people out of hours

Keep local

  • Judgement, negotiation and pricing decisions
  • Regulated or licensed activity
  • Relationship ownership with key customers
  • Physical, on-site delivery
Buy-and-build makes this stronger. A platform integrating add-ons duplicates back offices by definition. A shared offshore function across the group is one of the cleanest synergy cases available — and it is far easier to build once, centrally, than to unpick five inherited teams.

Three routes

How much the fund chooses to push

Require, sponsor or recommend

Funds differ in how directly they intervene in portfolio operations. All three postures work — what matters is choosing one deliberately rather than leaving it to chance.

Require

Written into the value-creation plan and the hundred-day plan.

The strongest position, available to control investors. Offshoring is a named workstream with an owner, a target and a reporting line. Best suited to platform acquisitions where the back office is being rebuilt anyway, and to companies acquired specifically for margin improvement. Highest certainty of delivery; requires the fund to carry the change-management load with management.

Sponsor

The fund brings the partner, the terms and the case; the company decides.

The most common posture. The fund negotiates a group arrangement, introduces it to each portfolio company, and lets management adopt it on their own timetable. Preserves CEO autonomy, spreads the commercial benefit across the portfolio, and creates internal reference cases that make later adoption easy. Adoption is slower but far more durable.

Recommend

A preferred supplier on the list, used when a company asks.

The lightest touch, appropriate for minority positions, venture portfolios and family offices. Costs the fund nothing, carries no governance burden, and still puts a vetted option in front of founders at the moment they hit an operational wall. Lowest certainty, but often where the relationship starts.

A practical sequenceMost firms begin at recommend, move to sponsor once one company has proved the model, and require it only in new platform acquisitions where the operating plan is being written from scratch.

What good looks like

The difference between offshoring that lifts EBITDA and offshoring that quietly costs more than it saves is almost never the talent. It is the operating model around the talent.

The managed model

Dedicated, not shared

One person, one company, one set of responsibilities — not a pooled resource answering tickets for six clients. Dedicated staff learn the business, and that learning is where the productivity comes from.

Employed, not contracted

Our people are permanent employees with careers, development and support. That is why they stay, and retention is the single biggest determinant of whether an offshore arrangement is still working in year three.

Supervised and covered

Local team leadership, quality assurance, and cover for leave and illness. The portfolio company should never feel the operational risk of one individual having a bad month.

Inside your systems

Staff work in the company’s own systems under its own access controls and processes — not in a parallel environment that has to be reconciled later.

Governance the fund should expect

AreaStandard
ConfidentialityIndividual NDAs, role-based access, and no data held outside the company’s own systems.
Security postureManaged, access-controlled facility; device and network controls; auditable on request.
Service definitionA written scope per role, agreed output standards, and named escalation contacts.
ReportingMonthly performance reporting to the company, aggregated to the fund where the fund sponsors the arrangement.
ExitDocumented processes and a defined transition path, so the company is never trapped.
Ask this question of any provider. “Who employs the person, who manages them day to day, and what happens when they resign?” The answers separate a managed capability from a labour marketplace.

Risk

Five failure modes, and the fix for each

Why offshoring fails — and how to avoid it

Scepticism in this market is earned. Nearly every investor has seen an offshoring arrangement disappoint. In our experience the causes are consistent, well understood and avoidable.

Failure modeHow to avoid it
Scope was never definedWrite the role down before recruiting: tasks, volumes, systems, output standards and who reviews the work. Vague scopes produce vague results.
Nobody owned it locallyName one person in the portfolio company accountable for the relationship. Offshore capability fails fastest when it has no local sponsor.
Processes were undocumentedIf the work only exists in one person’s head, document it first. This is worth doing regardless — it de-risks the company at exit too.
Cheapest option chosenHourly marketplaces optimise for price, not retention. The cost of churn and rework routinely exceeds the difference in rate.
Treated as a headcount cutFrame it as capacity for growth. Teams that believe the intent is redundancy will not transfer knowledge, and the arrangement stalls.

The question is not whether offshore talent can do the work. It is whether your organisation has defined the work well enough for anyone to do it.

The honest caveatNot every portfolio company is a candidate, and a good partner will tell you which ones are not. We would rather decline a poor-fit engagement than deliver one that damages the reference case for the rest of your portfolio.

How to start

Do not begin with a portfolio-wide programme. Begin with one role, in one company, measured over one quarter. It is cheap to try, quick to judge, and becomes the internal proof point that makes every subsequent deployment straightforward.

A ninety-day pilot

1

Days 1–10 · Choose the company and the role

Pick a portfolio company with an obvious administrative load and a willing management team. Define one role, its tasks, its systems and the measures of success.

2

Days 10–30 · Recruit and prepare

We recruit to the written brief and shortlist for the company to interview. In parallel the company documents the processes and confirms access and security arrangements.

3

Days 30–60 · Onboard and stabilise

Structured onboarding into the company’s systems and standards, with our supervision alongside the local owner. Output quality is reviewed weekly rather than assumed.

4

Days 60–90 · Measure and decide

Assess against the measures set on day one: work delivered, quality, capacity released locally, and net cost effect. Then decide — extend, expand within the company, or stop.
